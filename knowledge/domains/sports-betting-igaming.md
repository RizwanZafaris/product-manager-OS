# Sports betting and iGaming

A sportsbook or online casino product sells a bet: a wager on a sporting or random outcome for a cash prize, taken online, through an app, or at a licensed retail counter, and the scope runs from sportsbooks and online casino to daily fantasy, lottery and sweepstakes-style products and the B2B platforms behind them. This domain differs from [Gaming](gaming.md), the row a sportsbook PM reaches for by habit and gets wrong, on three facts: the licence is per jurisdiction and decides whether the product may exist there at all, geolocation decides whether this specific bet may be placed right now, and the operator carries trading risk on every market it prices, win or lose. Gaming's certification duty and session loop still apply, but responsible-gambling duty sits under a second, heavier gate here: a licence condition reaching into deposit limits, reality checks, and stake caps, not a feature choice.

## Questions a PM must ask

1. Which jurisdictions is this product licensed in, and what does each licence actually approve: the operator, this specific product, these bet types, sometimes even this release? Legal model matters too, a state gaming licence, a sweepstakes (no purchase necessary) structure, or a federally regulated event contract, since US legality runs state by state after *Murphy v. National Collegiate Athletic Association*, 584 U.S. 453 (2018), and each model faces active challenge, for example California's AB 831 (signed 11 October 2025, creating Penal Code section 337o, in force since 1 January 2026), which bans dual-currency sweepstakes casinos outright and extends misdemeanor liability to any vendor, payment processor, or geolocation provider that knowingly supports one (as of 2026-09-11; confirm with counsel).
2. Where is the player right now, and what does the product do when geolocation cannot tell? A licence is a licence for a place, not a person, so a bet from outside the approved footprint is an offence regardless of intent, and the honest default when ambiguous is to fail closed, never to fail open and hope. The hard cases are a spoofed location, a VPN, a mocked GPS signal, and a player near a state border; the check has to detect spoofing, not just read the device's reported location.
3. Who must be verified and screened before a deposit or first bet, not after? Age and identity verification clears before real-money play starts, and the account is checked against the self-exclusion registers in that market, GAMSTOP in Great Britain and the equivalent scheme elsewhere, before it can fund.
4. Which responsible-gambling duties are product surfaces rather than legal footnotes? Deposit limits, reality checks, financial vulnerability checks, and Great Britain's online-slot stake caps under the Gambling Act 2005 (Operating Licence Conditions) (Amendment) Regulations 2025 (SI 2025/215), £5 a spin for players 25 and over from 9 April 2025 and £2 a spin for players 18 to 24 from 21 May 2025 (as of 2026-09-11; confirm with counsel), are UI decisions with a named regulator behind them, and a product that treats them as a compliance appendix ships them late or wrong.
5. How does money actually move, and will a banking partner carry this business? Gambling carries its own merchant category codes with high-risk underwriting behind each (see Gatekeepers below), Great Britain has banned credit-card gambling since 14 April 2020, and deposits above a risk threshold trigger AML source-of-funds checks in the same flow as the card and bank decisions (as of 2026-09-11; confirm with counsel); a slow payout is not only a support ticket, it can be a compliance hold, and either way it is churn.
6. What does a promotion actually cost once redeemed? A bonus sits between handle and net gaming revenue, not on top of it; bonus abuse, multi-accounting and matched-offer arbitrage, is a live fraud vector, and advertising codes and state statutory limits constrain what a promotion may say.
7. Who carries the risk on the line the trading desk set? An operator that limits or bans winning bettors protects its margin and invites fairness scrutiny; in-play markets add latency risk, and some US states mandate in-play data come from a licensed official league feed, not whichever source is fastest (as of 2026-09-11; confirm with counsel).
8. What integrity duties does the product owe beyond its own player base? Suspicious betting is reported to the regulator and, where required, the sport's governing body, not contracted integrity monitors, the statutory recipients are named in Gatekeepers below (as of 2026-09-11; confirm with counsel). An in-play product built on unlicensed or unofficial data is a licence problem before it is a data-quality one.

## Gatekeepers

- **Gaming regulators, one per jurisdiction.** US state gaming and racing commissions, the UK Gambling Commission, the Malta Gaming Authority, and, in Ontario, AGCO (the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario), which registers operators and sets the Registrar's Standards for Internet Gaming; iGaming Ontario, a standalone provincial agency under the iGaming Ontario Act, 2024 since 12 May 2025 (an AGCO subsidiary before that), holds the operating agreement (confirm current status with counsel). Each can approve, condition, suspend, or revoke a licence; a material change can trigger re-approval.
- **Geolocation, identity, and self-exclusion providers.** The technical layer a licence depends on. New Jersey requires the system to detect a patron's physical location and block play outside it, at N.J.A.C. 13:69O-1.2(e) for internet casino gaming and N.J.A.C. 13:69N-1.2(g) for online sports wagering (current as of 2026-09-11; confirm before relying), and a false negative here is the operator's offence, not the vendor's.
- **Card networks, acquirers, and banks.** Gambling carries its own merchant category codes: 7995 for betting generally, including most online sportsbooks, and in the US 7801 for government-licensed online casino (internet gambling), 7800 for government lotteries, and 7802 for licensed horse and dog racing; the acquirer assigns the final code and each draws high-risk underwriting (as of 2026-09-11; confirm with the acquirer and counsel). Great Britain's credit-card gambling ban removes a payment method outright, and a bank can decline the merchant relationship on appetite alone, licence notwithstanding.
- **Leagues, official data providers, and integrity monitors.** Some US states require in-play data from a licensed official source; suspicious-betting reports go to the regulator and, where required, the sport's governing body, not integrity bodies as the statutory recipient (as of 2026-09-11; confirm with counsel), and a data-feed dispute can pull markets mid-event.
- **App-store and advertising policy.** Apple App Store Review Guidelines section 5.3, Gaming, Gambling, and Lotteries (current as of 2026-09-11), requires the app to be licensed and geo-restricted to where it is legal, and advertising regulators constrain what a promotion can claim; either can pull a build or a campaign with no engineering defect involved.

## Metrics that matter

| Metric | What it tells you | How it lies |
|---|---|---|
| Handle | Volume of money wagered | Grows with promotional free bets and odds boosts while margin underneath it shrinks |
| Gross gaming revenue (GGR) | Handle minus payouts, the house's raw win | A strong GGR week can still be a loss after bonus spend |
| Net gaming revenue (NGR) | GGR minus promotional cost, closer to the real number | The taxable definition differs by jurisdiction, so a number without a stated definition is not comparable to last quarter's |
| Hold percentage | Trading margin on handle | Swings with sporting results for weeks; trend it over a season, not a week |
| Promotions as a share of GGR | Real cost of acquisition and retention | A falling ratio can mean discipline, or bonus abuse finally got expensive enough to write off |
| Geolocation check failure rate and false-block rate | Whether the fail-closed control actually works | A low failure rate can mean an over-permissive check waving borderline signals through |
| RG interventions and self-exclusions per 1,000 active accounts | Whether the RG programme is doing anything | A rise can mean better detection or more harm occurring; read it against player outcomes, never alone |

## Reading

- **Natasha Dow Schüll, *Addiction by Design: Machine Gambling in Las Vegas* (Princeton University Press, 2012).** An ethnography of slot-machine design built around what machine gamblers themselves call the zone, a state of absorbed play the machine's pacing and near-miss patterns are engineered to produce. Read it as the argument that interface choices this domain treats as UX polish, bet speed, autoplay, near-miss feedback, are the mechanism, not a side effect of one.
- **UK Department for Culture, Media and Sport, *High stakes: gambling reform for the digital age* (Command Paper CP 835, presented to Parliament 27 April 2023).** The policy document behind Great Britain's online-slot stake limits, in force since April/May 2025, and its £150 net-deposits-over-30-days financial vulnerability checks, in force since 28 February 2025. The heavier, credit-reference-based enhanced financial risk assessments remain a staged pilot rollout following the Gambling Commission's 7 July 2026 announcement ("Commission to introduce Financial Risk Assessments in staged approach", https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/commission-to-introduce-financial-risk-assessments-in-staged-approach); the assessment is frictionless and document-free, provided by credit reference agencies with no impact on credit score. Stage one, at the largest operators, will apply where there is high spend of multiple thousands of pounds over 24 hours, for most customers meaning a net deposit of £5,000 in a rolling 24-hour period; no stage has a binding start date yet, and during the early stages no enforcement action will be taken on a failure to act following an assessment. Once fully implemented, the thresholds will be £1,000 in a rolling 24 hours or £3,000 over a rolling 90 days for customers aged 25 or over, and £750 in 24 hours or £2,000 in 90 days for under-25s (as of 2026-09-11; confirm with counsel). Read the reasoning, not only the numbers: it is the clearest public record of a regulator weighing product design against measured harm evidence.
