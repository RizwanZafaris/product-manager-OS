# Accessibility Regulation

Compiled from the primary and near-primary texts named in Reading, this card sets the legal floor experience design must clear. It is not the DESIGN stage or Gate 3 in [os/STAGE-GATES.md](../../os/STAGE-GATES.md), and it is not legal advice.

## The essence

Status as of 2026-09-10: 2 of 10 rows below are verified against the source text fetched directly from the issuing body; the remaining eight are marked secondary or UNVERIFIED, and each says why in its own row. This is not legal advice. Every row needs confirmation from counsel before it sets a launch date or a conformance target.

A product picks a conformance target once, in [nfr.md](../../templates/definition/nfr.md) section 5, and that choice is either grounded in a jurisdiction's actual law or it is a guess wearing a percentage sign. This card exists because that grounding takes real digging: a directive's exemption article, a standard's version number, a rule's extension notice, a press release that may or may not be law. The table below is that digging, done once, dated, and marked by how sure the underlying text makes a reader. Two things make an obligation map different from most knowledge cards here. First, it decays on a clock nobody controls: a comment period closes, a standard gets cited in an official journal, a rule gets extended, and the row that was accurate in September is wrong in November. Second, unlike a heuristic or a framework, getting a row wrong does not cost a sprint, it costs a regulator's attention. Both are reasons this card names a review trigger for every row instead of asking a reader to trust a single "current as of" date at the top.

## Where it came from

The European Accessibility Act, Directive (EU) 2019/882, was adopted in 2019 with a transposition deadline of 28 June 2022 and an application date of 28 June 2025 for the products and services it lists, both traced to EUR-Lex (secondary). The EU Web Accessibility Directive, 2016/2102, predates it by three years and covers only public sector websites and apps; it cites EN 301 549 v3.2.1 for presumption of conformity, via Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/1339. The EAA itself does not yet cite a version of EN 301 549 in the Official Journal; v3.2.1 is the working reference for it too, per AccessibleEU, pending that citation. Both directives draw on the same standard family, EN 301 549, maintained jointly by ETSI, CEN and CENELEC, which has followed WCAG upward through its own version numbers since the standard's inception. In the United States, Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act is the older instrument, last technically refreshed by the US Access Board in 2017 to harmonise federal ICT procurement with WCAG 2.0 and with EN 301 549; the Americans with Disabilities Act's Title II only gained a specific technical rule for state and local government web content in 2024, and that rule's compliance dates were already extended once, in 2026, before this card was written. The UK's Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations came into force in 2018, originally transposing the EU's WAD, and now stand as domestic UK law after Brexit; the Equality Act 2010 sits beside it as a general duty with no named technical standard. The UAE's National Digital Accessibility Policy is the newest of the government mandates here, announced by the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority on 6 May 2024. Pakistan's ICT Rights of Persons with Disability Act, 2020, gazetted in September 2020, is the outlier: a general disability-rights statute with one clause on websites and no named technical standard at all.

## When to use it

- Setting the conformance target in [nfr.md](../../templates/definition/nfr.md) section 5, before a designer or an engineer has a number to build against.
- Filling section 2 of a [compliance impact assessment](../../templates/operate/compliance-impact-assessment.md), where a specific jurisdiction row becomes an "applies because" entry with evidence and a named holder.
- Scoping which markets a launch actually touches, before a stakeholder's confident deadline gets written into a plan unread.
- Deciding whether a claimed exemption, a microenterprise size, a legacy self-service terminal, a documented disproportionate-burden case, actually applies to your product rather than to the neighbouring one in the same article.

**Skip it when:** the product ships in none of the ten jurisdictions below, or when counsel or a compliance officer has already supplied a binding target for this launch. This card does not override a named human who has read the current text; it exists for the far more common case where nobody has yet.

## Gaps

This map does not cover the EAA's Annex V information duties, the accessibility statement and compliance information a provider must publish alongside a product or service. It does not cover national penalty regimes for non-compliance, which the EAA leaves to each Member State to set individually. It does not cover UK private-sector duties beyond the general Equality Act 2010 reasonable-adjustments row, which is itself UNVERIFIED above. It does not cover any planned update to the US Section 508 technical standard beyond the 2017 refresh. A jurisdiction not listed here, including most of the GCC beyond the two UNVERIFIED rows, most of Africa, and most of Latin America, is missing by omission, not by a finding that no accessibility law applies there.

## The practical rule

The standards in this table that currently carry presumption of conformity or statutory force, EN 301 549 v3.2.1, the ADA's WCAG 2.1 AA, and Section 508's WCAG 2.0 AA baseline, are written against WCAG 2.1 or an earlier version. WCAG 2.2 keeps every user-facing barrier that 2.0 and 2.1 test for: it added six new success criteria at Level A and AA (2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured Minimum, 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, 2.5.8 Target Size Minimum at 24 by 24 CSS pixels, 3.2.6 Consistent Help, 3.3.7 Redundant Entry, 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication Minimum) plus three at AAA, and removed only one criterion, 4.1.1 Parsing, a markup-validation check the working group judged obsolete because assistive technology no longer parses HTML directly, not a barrier a person actually meets (see W3C, "What's New in WCAG 2.2"). Call it a functional superset, not a literal one: a product conforming to 2.2 AA is not tested against 4.1.1, but nothing a real person would hit is left uncovered by dropping it. WCAG 2.2 AA satisfies the WCAG-based obligations in this table (ADA Title II, Section 508's web baseline, and clause 9 of EN 301 549); EN 301 549 also carries non-web clauses (documentation and support services, software, hardware) that WCAG does not test, so a product in the EU rows needs those clauses walked separately. Setting the conformance target in nfr.md section 5 at 2.2 AA is the practical default: it clears every legal floor in this table today, and it clears the floor EN 301 549 v4.1.1 is moving toward once cited. The practice of meeting that target, as opposed to the law requiring it, is the sibling card `knowledge/design/accessibility-and-inclusive-design.md`.

## The trap: reading a standard's existence as a standard's force

EN 301 549 v4.1.1 exists. It was published in September 2026 (reported by AccessibleEU), names WCAG 2.2, and even carries an annex mapping itself to the EAA. It is tempting to treat it as this year's legal answer. It is not: only v3.2.1, keyed to WCAG 2.1 AA, gives presumption of conformity, and only until the European Commission formally cites v4.1.1 in the Official Journal. A team that ships to v4.1.1 alone and calls the row above satisfied has confused a standard's publication with a standard's citation. The trap runs the other way too. Article 4(5)'s microenterprise exemption in the EAA covers service providers only, never product manufacturers, and it requires genuinely meeting the size test in Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC, not just feeling small. A ten-person hardware team that reads the exemption and skips accessibility on a self-service terminal has misapplied a services-only exemption to a product, and carries the full Article 14 burden of a documented disproportionate-burden assessment instead of the exemption it assumed it had.

## How it lies

A dated table looks finished the day it is written and starts decaying the same day. Three decay modes sit in this table right now. A standard can exist without yet carrying legal force, which is the EN 301 549 v4.1.1 case above. A compliance date already extended once by an Interim Final Rule can extend again, since the ADA's own comment period only closed 22 June 2026 and no final rule had issued as of this card's compile date. And a jurisdiction can look identical to a reader skimming the table to one with real law behind it, when the Saudi Arabia and Qatar rows in fact rest on a single vendor's secondary summary, because the government's own accessibility page returned blocked on the attempt to fetch it directly. The verified-against-primary-text column exists precisely to stop that last failure mode; read it before the compliance date, not after, because a date read without that column's context is a number wearing the coat of a fact.
