# PM Specializations

The [ladder](ladder.md) describes altitude; specialization describes terrain. Same rungs, same gate discipline, different problems and different documents. Seven shapes recur across the industry, described below with what each owns, decides, and produces, its success measure, and the specific way it goes wrong. Titles vary by company even more here than on the ladder, so map by the work, not the badge.

One rule holds across all seven: specialization narrows the problems, never the accountability. A Growth PM who disclaims responsibility for retention quality, or a Platform PM who disclaims usability because the users are engineers, has traded the job for a fraction of it.

## Core PM

The default shape and the reference point for the other six: one product area, all six stages of the [operating loop](../../os/OPERATING-LOOP.md), all four risks of [empowered product teams](../cagan-product-teams.md).

- **Decides:** scope, priority, and document weight within the area; gate recommendations.
- **Documents out:** the [discovery document](../../templates/discovery/discovery-document.md), the head of the chain that runs from discovery to the metrics review, names the Gate 1 success signal before any solution exists; a Product Owner starts from the PRD and never writes this, and a Growth PM starts from a metric rather than a problem. The [PRD](../../templates/definition/prd.md) carries all four risks with counter-evidence at Gate 2, because the Core PM is the shape that keeps every one of them; a Technical PM's PRD is a companion to an API contract and an AI PM's acceptance criteria become eval rows. The [design brief](../../templates/definition/design-brief.md) is the agreement between product and design on problem, users, constraints, and done, the Core PM's half of the usability seat in the triad; a Product Owner relays it and a Platform PM's users are engineers who rarely get one. The [GTM plan](../../templates/planning/gtm-plan.md) is deliberately a product document at the PMM boundary, with first cohort, channel evidence, launch metric, and stop condition, written by the PM rather than by product marketing; a Platform PM's launches are internal and a Data PM's are migrations. The chain ends at the [metrics review](../../templates/operate/metrics-review.md), the Gate 6 read that verifies the outcome named at Gate 1; a Growth PM consumes it to pick the next bet and a Data PM builds the dashboard it reads from.
- **Documents in:** consumes [product strategy](../../templates/planning/product-strategy.md) and [vision](../../templates/planning/vision.md), because the Gate 1 problem must be one the strategy said to pursue; [OKRs](../../templates/planning/okrs.md), because the key results are the targets Gate 6 will verify; [positioning](../../templates/planning/positioning.md), reviewed for accuracy rather than taste at the PMM boundary, since it decides who the GTM plan aims at; [evidence notes](../../templates/discovery/evidence-note.md), because every claim in the discovery document cites one with a verbatim quote, read before the document is written; and the [north star metric](../../templates/planning/north-star-metric.md), so the Gate 1 success signal is an input to it rather than a private definition of winning.
- **Recurring workflows:** per product pass, the [conductor](../../skills/conductor/SKILL.md) runs the six stages as an interview, the Core PM's terrain by definition; per discovery pass, [persona builder](../../skills/persona-builder/SKILL.md) writes job stories first, then personas whose every attribute traces to an evidence note, then the journey and the opportunity tree; per customer-facing release, [GTM launch planner](../../skills/gtm-launch-planner/SKILL.md) sets the launch tier by rule, names a cohort you can list, and drafts the channel plan so Gate 5 finds it written; per release, [launch readiness](../../skills/launch-readiness/SKILL.md) walks the eight Gate 5 items against the release candidate rather than slides.
- **Success measure:** the outcome named at Gate 1, verified at Gate 6.
- **Failure mode:** shapelessness. Without a deliberate terrain, a core PM drifts to wherever the loudest stakeholder points.

## Product Owner

The role Scrum defines: order the backlog, accept the work, be available to the team. Marty Cagan's position, argued across the SVPG essays, is that carving this off as a separate junior job is an anti-pattern: it splits the person who decides from the person who saw the evidence, and produces a backlog administrator with no mandate to say no. This repository takes the same side: PO is a set of responsibilities a PM carries into delivery, not a separate career.

The counter-reality: large Scrum and SAFe organizations staff the split anyway, and holding the PO title is not a character flaw. If that is your seat, the card's advice is to do the PM job from it.

- **Decides:** backlog order, sprint-level acceptance, clarifications the team needs today.
- **Documents out:** the [user story register](../../templates/definition/user-stories.md), with permanent ids and the release slice, is the PO's, because backlog order and sprint-level acceptance are the PO's decisions; a Core PM owns the PRD the stories decompose and an APM owns only a feature's stories. [Acceptance criteria](../../templates/definition/acceptance-criteria.md), the given/when/then rows with thresholds, are theirs to write and accept against, because accepted work matching written criteria is the PO's success measure; a Core PM signs scope and an AI PM's criteria become eval rows instead. [Business rules](../../templates/definition/business-rules.md), the eligibility, limits, and approvals the organization already decided, are the PO's to record with test traceability; a Core PM decides scope and a Technical PM decides contract shape, neither transcribes the rulebook. The [backlog](../../templates/execution/backlog.md) queue policy, naming what may enter, the one ranking method, and the kill policy, is the PO's instrument against proxy management; a Platform PM's backlog policy sorts internal requests into roadmap or tickets, a different queue. The [UAT plan](../../templates/delivery/uat-plan.md) carries the PO's sign-off, because accepting the work is their seat; a Core PM certifies readiness for the market at Gate 5, the PO certifies that what was built matches the criteria.
- **Documents in:** consumes the [PRD](../../templates/definition/prd.md), because it is the scope the backlog decomposes and must not be edited to hold stories, and the [FRD](../../templates/definition/frd.md), because the functional requirements with traceability back to PRD items are what the stories and business rules trace to in both directions; and the [change request](../../templates/execution/change-request.md), because an approved change to the signed baseline is what forces a re-order of the backlog, so the PO reads every one before moving the queue.
- **Recurring workflows:** per approved PRD and per sprint, [story writer](../../skills/story-writer/SKILL.md) decomposes along the journey into INVEST-checked stories with criteria a tester can run; per release, the [user story map](../../frameworks/prioritization/user-story-map.md) arranges stories along the journey so the first release is a working slice rather than a pile of finished parts; per fixed-date negotiation, [MoSCoW](../../frameworks/prioritization/moscow.md) caps the musts so scope against a date is a negotiation with arithmetic rather than a relayed demand.
- **Success measure:** the team is never blocked on a product answer, and accepted work matches written criteria.
- **Failure mode:** proxy management: relaying decisions made elsewhere at increasing speed and decreasing fidelity. The escape is to start producing discovery evidence nobody asked for, which is how POs become PMs.

## Technical PM

A PM whose users or buyers are engineers: APIs, developer tools, infrastructure sold or consumed as product. One title collision to defuse immediately: Technical Program Manager is a different job (cross-team delivery orchestration) that shares the acronym; this entry covers the product role.

- **Decides:** API surface and contract shape jointly with architects, versioning and deprecation policy, which integrations are supported versus tolerated.
- **Documents out:** the [API contract](../../templates/architecture/api-contract.md) is the surface that consumers the team will never meet build against, exactly as written, decided jointly with architects; a Core PM's users never see an endpoint, and a Platform PM's consumers are on the payroll and can be migrated. The [NFR](../../templates/definition/nfr.md) carries the Technical PM's success measure as written targets, every latency, availability, and scale line with a number or a named owner; a Core PM fills the NFR as a companion, a Technical PM's users buy the numbers. The [integration register](../../templates/architecture/integrations.md) records which integrations are supported versus tolerated, with owner, protocol, SLA, and failure behavior per line; a Platform PM's integration register faces inward to consuming teams instead. The Technical PM co-signs [ADRs](../../templates/architecture/adr.md) behind the contract surface, because versioning and deprecation policy are contested, expensive-to-reverse decisions; a Principal writes ADRs for cross-cutting bets and a Core PM reads them as constraints. For a product whose users are builders, the Technical PM co-authors [system design](../../templates/architecture/system-design.md)'s goals, non-goals, and alternatives rather than receiving them, because for this shape the design is primary reading, not an appendix; a Core PM treats the design as an appendix. The [SLA](../../templates/delivery/sla-slo-definition.md), promised externally and kept deliberately looser than the SLO, is the customer-facing form of the NFR targets the Technical PM owns; a Platform PM sets SLOs for internal teams without an external SLA and a Data PM sets freshness thresholds in observability instead.
- **Documents in:** consumes the [sequence diagram](../../templates/architecture/sequence-diagram.md), because a flow that crosses a system boundary is what a builder-user experiences, read before the contract is written; the [data model](../../templates/architecture/data-model.md), because the entities and keys a contract exposes outlive the code; the [security architecture](../../templates/architecture/security-architecture.md), because trust boundaries per component constrain an API's auth and error design, read rather than delegated; and the [tech debt register](../../templates/execution/tech-debt-register.md), because documentation debt is the card's visible symptom of the failure mode, priced there every quarter.
- **Recurring workflows:** per major contract change, [FMEA](../../frameworks/execution/fmea.md) walks the system function by function for every way each step can fail and what would catch it, before the NFR target is set; quarterly, [DORA four keys](../../frameworks/metrics/dora-four-keys.md) measures the delivery health of a product whose adopters judge it by deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, and time to restore; per API contract or NFR heading to a gate, [spec review](../../skills/spec-review/SKILL.md) finds the unquantified adjective and the requirement that cannot fail, where fluency without judgment shows up.
- **Success measure:** adoption by builders and time-to-first-successful-call; reliability against the written NFR targets.
- **Failure mode:** fluency without judgment: knowing the system well enough to defend every constraint and forgetting to represent the user who meets it. Documentation debt is the visible symptom.

## Growth PM

Owns a metric, not a surface: activation, retention, monetization, or the loop that connects them. Works in experiment portfolios where most bets lose. The craft descends from Sean Ellis's growth practice and the loops-over-funnels framing argued by Brian Balfour and Casey Winters; the [north star metric](../north-star-metric.md) card and the AARRR entry in the [knowledge index](../README.md) carry the underlying models.

- **Decides:** the experiment portfolio and its kill thresholds, which input metric is the current bet, when a result is real versus noise.
- **Documents out:** the [growth plan](../../templates/planning/growth-plan.md) carries the input-metric bet, the counter-metric, and the kill condition, the card's named decisions; a Core PM plans a surface and a Data PM plans instrumentation. Each [experiment brief](../../templates/operate/experiment-brief.md) in the portfolio writes its decision rule before the result, because the experiment portfolio and its kill thresholds are the Growth PM's decisions; a Core PM runs an experiment to buy a decision, a Growth PM runs a portfolio where most bets lose. The Growth PM owns the rows it bets on in the [metrics dictionary](../../templates/operate/metrics-dictionary.md), one definition per metric that two people compute to the same number, because deciding when a result is real versus noise requires it; a Data PM owns the dashboard tiles that cite those rows and the pipelines beneath them.
- **Documents in:** consumes the [metrics review](../../templates/operate/metrics-review.md), because the input-metric section is where the next bet comes from; the [analytics instrumentation spec](../../templates/delivery/analytics-instrumentation-spec.md), because an experiment on an event nobody emits has no baseline; the [north star metric](../../templates/planning/north-star-metric.md) tree, where the Growth PM's branch lives and the guardrails are the counter-metrics the growth plan must honor; and the [dashboard spec](../../templates/operate/dashboard-spec.md), because the tiles and drill-downs the Data PM built are what a result is read from, and the spec says what can and cannot be seen.
- **Recurring workflows:** per bet, [experiment designer](../../skills/experiment-designer/SKILL.md) states a mechanism, one primary metric, guardrails with floors, a sample size from the minimum detectable effect, and the decision rule before anyone sees a result; per loop, [growth loops](../../frameworks/metrics/growth-loops.md) draws where output becomes the next input and does its arithmetic, the loops-over-funnels framing the card descends from; quarterly, [AARRR](../../frameworks/metrics/aarrr-funnel.md) defines each stage by its event and finds where the funnel leaks, so the current bet is placed at the leak rather than at the step in front of you; per cohort read, [cohort retention](../../frameworks/metrics/cohort-retention.md) checks whether the curve flattens and avoids the standard misreads before a retention win is booked.
- **Success measure:** input metrics moved with the counter-metric honored. A growth win that degrades the counter-metric is a loss booked in a different quarter.
- **Failure mode:** local maxima and dark patterns: optimizing the step in front of you until the product is a funnel wearing a product costume. The kill condition in the growth plan template exists because of this failure.

## Platform PM

Runs an internal platform as a product where the customers are the company's own teams, and captive customers make every signal a liar.

- **Decides:** what the paved road includes, migration timelines and deprecation dates, which team requests are roadmap and which are tickets.
- **Documents out:** the [API contract](../../templates/architecture/api-contract.md) writes what the paved road includes as a contract internal teams build against; the Technical PM's contract faces external adopters who can leave, which changes every signal. The [integration register](../../templates/architecture/integrations.md) faces inward, with every consuming team's line across the platform boundary carrying an owner, an SLA, and a failure behavior, because captive customers make every signal a liar; the Technical PM's faces the counterparties it sells to. The [partner integration brief](../../templates/planning/partner-integration-brief.md) is the go or no-go for building on a partner, with owners on both sides of the integration surface; a Core PM proposes the partnership, the Platform PM writes the brief that says whether the road can carry it. The [dependency register](../../templates/execution/dependency-register.md) is actually governed, because every consuming team's date sits on the platform's; a Senior PM's register holds outbound dependencies on other teams, the Platform PM's holds every team's inbound dependency on them. Migration timelines and deprecation dates are the Platform PM's decision, written as the phased sequence with rehearsals and the point of no return in the [migration cutover plan](../../templates/delivery/migration-cutover-plan.md); a Principal's cutover is one cross-cutting bet, the Platform PM's is the recurring paved-road migration. The [backlog](../../templates/execution/backlog.md) queue policy decides which team requests are roadmap and which are tickets; a Product Owner's queue policy orders one team's stories.
- **Documents in:** consumes every consuming team's [roadmap](../../templates/planning/roadmap.md), because that is where the dependency register's dates come from. The consuming teams' own [system designs](../../templates/architecture/system-design.md) are read first, to learn what duct tape the paved road has to beat. And the [tech debt register](../../templates/execution/tech-debt-register.md), because the compromises consuming teams carry to avoid the platform are the evidence of an empty paved road, priced per quarter.
- **Recurring workflows:** quarterly, [theory of constraints](../../frameworks/execution/theory-of-constraints.md) finds the one station that sets the pace of every consuming team's delivery line, where a platform's roadmap earns voluntary adoption; quarterly, the [SPACE framework](../../frameworks/metrics/space-framework.md) runs a productivity slate across five dimensions for internal engineer-users, so mandated adoption is never reported as if it were product-market fit; before every cutover, [program premortem](../../skills/program-premortem/SKILL.md) checks the twelve failure modes against a migration with three or more consuming teams, before the published date is committed.
- **Success measure:** voluntary adoption, or where adoption is mandated, migration completed on the published date with support load falling afterward. Mandated adoption is not product-market fit and must never be reported as if it were.
- **Failure mode:** building for the architecture review instead of the internal user. The platform is elegant, the paved road is empty, and product teams keep their own duct tape because the duct tape ships.

## Data PM

Owns data products: pipelines, metrics layers, instrumentation standards, the datasets other teams build on. The user is anyone who makes a decision from the data, which is everyone.

- **Decides:** schema contracts and their change policy, data quality thresholds and who is paged on breach, what gets instrumented as standard versus per-team.
- **Documents out:** the [analytics instrumentation spec](../../templates/delivery/analytics-instrumentation-spec.md) decides what gets instrumented as standard versus per-team, with the event taxonomy, owners, and a QA plan written before BUILD; a Growth PM consumes it and a Core PM only names the metrics it must emit. The [observability spec](../../templates/architecture/observability.md) sets data quality thresholds and who is paged on breach, with SLOs, alert thresholds, and the dashboard owner set before code exists; a Technical PM's thresholds are on an API, the Data PM's are on the datasets everyone builds on. The [dashboard spec](../../templates/operate/dashboard-spec.md) names who looks, what question each tile answers, and where it drills, because trust means decisions made from the governed dataset instead of private spreadsheets; the metric definitions it cites belong to the Growth PM and the other metric owners. The [data model](../../templates/architecture/data-model.md) carries schema contracts and their change policy, with entities, keys, and PII classification that outlive the code that uses them; a Technical PM reads the model to shape a contract, the Data PM owns the model itself. The [privacy impact assessment](../../templates/architecture/privacy-impact-assessment.md) structures the processing description, necessity questions, and risks to the individuals whose data it is, because data products inherit every regulation their inputs touch; an AI PM's privacy risk is the model's, a Data PM's is the pipeline's.
- **Documents in:** consumes the [compliance impact assessment](../../templates/operate/compliance-impact-assessment.md), because the data categories, retention, and DPIA flag constrain every schema; the [metrics dictionary](../../templates/operate/metrics-dictionary.md), because the metric definitions the dashboard tiles cite are owned by the metric owners and read to know what each tile must compute; every [PRD](../../templates/definition/prd.md), because the metrics it names are only measurable if the events exist; and the [NFR](../../templates/definition/nfr.md), because retention and freshness targets arrive as lines with numbers the data model and observability thresholds must meet.
- **Recurring workflows:** when two teams report the same metric with different numbers, [metrics tree](../../skills/metrics-tree/SKILL.md) builds the chain from the north star to one definition per metric and a dashboard an audience can read in a minute; per Gate 6 and per metric dispute, [product analyst](../../skills/product-analyst/SKILL.md) runs the evidence pass that ends a dispute by citation instead of meeting, the card's success measure; per data quality breach that pages someone, [postmortem facilitator](../../skills/postmortem-facilitator/SKILL.md) reconstructs the timeline from recorded sources and reaches causes in the pipeline the team can change.
- **Success measure:** trust: decisions are made from the governed dataset instead of private spreadsheets, and metric disputes end by citation instead of meeting.
- **Failure mode:** the dashboard factory. Output is measured in charts shipped; nobody can name a decision any chart changed. The fix is the same evidence discipline the discovery templates enforce, pointed inward.

## AI PM

Owns a product with a model inside, which adds a risk class the other six shapes do not carry: the product is probabilistic, and its failures are discovered rather than designed. In this repository the terrain is concrete: the AI overlay activates, and the [templates/ai/](../../templates/ai/eval-spec.md) pack becomes required reading rather than optional.

- **Decides:** eval thresholds and what blocks ship, guardrail scope, where a human approval gate is non-negotiable, the acceptable failure taxonomy and its monitoring.
- **Documents out:** the [eval spec](../../templates/ai/eval-spec.md) turns "it should work well" into something that can stop a release, with eval thresholds and what blocks ship, the card's first named decision; every other specialization's acceptance criteria are prose that can fail, the AI PM's are rows with a dataset. [Guardrails](../../templates/ai/guardrails.md) scope each rail to an enforcement point and an owner; a Core PM has no probabilistic output to fence and a Data PM fences data quality, not behavior. [Human approval gates](../../templates/ai/human-approval-gates.md) name where a gate is non-negotiable, with trigger conditions, an approver role, and a timeout that never approves on silence; no other specialization ships an action that waits for a named human by design. The [red team review](../../templates/ai/red-team-review.md) is co-signed by the AI PM as build owner, after the red-team agent runs the attack and before Gate 5, because failures in this terrain are discovered rather than designed; a Technical PM's security walk is STRIDE on components, not injection through user text. The [model card](../../templates/ai/model-card.md) states intended use, explicit out-of-scope uses, and known limitations citing the eval spec, for someone outside the building team; a Data PM documents a dataset's provenance, not a model's segment variance. [Hallucination controls](../../templates/ai/hallucination-controls.md) write down the acceptable failure taxonomy and its monitoring, with the grounding source, abstain policy, and monitored error taxonomy; a Growth PM monitors a metric, the AI PM monitors a failure class.
- **Documents in:** consumes everything a Core PM consumes, plus [incident postmortems](../../templates/operate/incident-postmortem.md) as a first-class evidence source per the card, because the failure taxonomy is otherwise written after the incident; the [prompt structure](../../templates/ai/prompt-structure.md), because the versioned system prompt is production code that happens to be prose, and product behavior changes when its change log does; and the [agent architecture](../../templates/ai/agent-architecture.md), because tools are permissions, so the per-agent tool list and least-access check are read before any guardrail or approval gate can be scoped. Under a financial or data regulator, the [compliance impact assessment](../../templates/operate/compliance-impact-assessment.md) attaches at Gate 2, and the applicable regulations and DPIA flag are read before the eval spec is written.
- **Recurring workflows:** per spec where a model produces the output, [ai-prd](../../skills/ai-prd/SKILL.md) turns acceptance criteria into eval sets, non-determinism into a requirement, and guardrails into features with owners; whenever a financial or data regulator applies to the model feature, [reg-gap-check](../../skills/reg-gap-check/SKILL.md) runs the severity-ranked gap table through the regulated module before Gate 2, as the card names; per release, [launch readiness](../../skills/launch-readiness/SKILL.md) walks the Gate 5 items with the AI overlay lines, guardrails verified live in the release candidate and the kill switch tested rather than designed.
- **Success measure:** product outcomes and eval pass rates and contained failures, jointly. Any one alone is a partial grade.
- **Failure mode:** demo-driven development: the demo works, the eval spec is a stub, and the failure taxonomy is written after the incident. The eval-spec template blocks ship on failure precisely because this failure mode is the default.
