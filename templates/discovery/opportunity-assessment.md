## 1. The ten questions

1. **What problem does this solve?** [the customer problem, not the solution restated as a problem]
2. **Who has this problem?** [target market, precisely; "everyone" fails the question]
3. **How big is the opportunity?** [size, with the arithmetic shown or the source linked; "assumption" is a legal answer]
4. **What alternatives exist today?** [what these customers do now, including the spreadsheet and doing nothing]
5. **Why are we suited to win?** [the asset, capability, or position that favors us; "nothing yet" is a legal answer that changes the recommendation]
6. **Why now?** [the shift that opens the window, and the evidence it is real]
7. **How would we take it to market?** [the first channel and cohort, one sentence; hand-waves here become launch failures later]
8. **How will we measure success?** [the metric, and the [north star](../planning/north-star-metric.md) input it would feed]
9. **What is critical to get right?** [the one or two factors that decide the outcome, per the team's best judgment]
10. **Given all of the above, what do we recommend?** [go to discovery / no / watch and revisit on a named condition]

## Exit gate

This assessment has done its job when:

- [ ] All ten questions carry answers of one to three sentences, none left blank
- [ ] Every factual claim either links evidence or is marked assumption
- [ ] The alternatives answer includes what customers do today, not only named competitors
- [ ] The success metric traces to the north star tree or admits it cannot yet
- [ ] The recommendation is a decision (go, no, or revisit-on-condition), not "more analysis needed"
- [ ] A no-go is recorded with the same care as a go, so the idea does not return unexamined
