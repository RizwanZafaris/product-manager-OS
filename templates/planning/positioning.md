<!-- The method here is April Dunford's, from Obviously Awesome (2019), restated in
     this repository's own words. Her core correction to the usual practice: do not
     start from the market category. Start from what customers would actually do
     without you, work forward through what only you have and the value that proves
     out, find the customers who care most, and only then choose the category, as
     context that makes the value obvious. Category-first positioning inherits the
     category's leader as your comparison point; alternative-first positioning lets
     you choose the fight.

     Work the sections in order. Each one is only as good as the one before it, which
     is why every section carries an evidence column. The output feeds the GTM plan's
     positioning skeleton and any sales one-pager cut from it. -->
