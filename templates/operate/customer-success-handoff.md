# Customer Success Handoff: [product or feature name]

**Stage:** DELIVER (feeds [Gate 5: release readiness green](../../os/STAGE-GATES.md)); the plan it hands over runs into OPERATE
**Knowledge:** [knowledge index](../../knowledge/INDEX.md); the signals in section 3 come from the [customer health score](../../frameworks/metrics/customer-health-score.md) worksheet and the adoption events from [activation and churn diagnosis](../../frameworks/metrics/activation-churn-diagnosis.md)
**Skill:** [gtm-launch-planner](../../skills/gtm-launch-planner/SKILL.md), for sequencing adoption by segment; [launch-readiness](../../skills/launch-readiness/SKILL.md) walks this file into Gate 5

**What checks this.** On the default path, where you fill this copy in an editor and a named human signs the gate form, Gate 5 is signed by the release owner, the product owner, the operations or support lead and the regulatory owner (regulated products only), per [os/STAGE-GATES.md](../../os/STAGE-GATES.md), and editing the document afterwards does not stale that signature: no hash binds it to the bytes. If you are running the optional `pmos` runtime, `python3 tools/init_product.py <product> --add` has already stamped this copy's artifact block from the frontmatter above, and approving Gate 5 with `pmos gate --bank-id deliver` records the copy's revision, so a later edit does stale that approval until the gate is proved again. Either way, no tool checks whether what you write is right; the people who sign the gate do. This paragraph is generated by `python3 tools/what_checks.py`.

> **When this is the wrong document.** A release with no change a customer can see needs a row in the [support runbook](../delivery/support-runbook.md) known-issues table and nothing here. A change that alters one support answer and no customer workflow belongs in the runbook too. A product with no customer success function, sold and served entirely self-serve, keeps sections 1, 5, 6 and 7 and writes "no account owners" in sections 2 and 4 rather than inventing owners. ## 8. How this handoff fails

<!-- Each row is a way the handoff fails while this document exists and
     looks filled. Read it before the handoff meeting, not after. -->

| Failure | The tell | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Handoff by forwarded release note | The receiving leads' names are blank, or were filled in after launch | Hold the meeting before Gate 5 with both leads present, and record its date in the header |
| Adoption target is "usage" | Section 2 has a percentage with no event and no date | Name the action from the activation diagnosis, and a metric id |
| Save plays on accounts that are only switching over | Falling flags cluster in launch week for accounts in the rollout wave | Section 3 suppression window with an end date, and the affected signal named |
| Real risk hidden inside the suppression window | A suppressed account's score is still falling after the end date | The end date is a review trigger, not a reset; read every suppressed account on that date |
| The CRM red list relabelled as launch risk | No row in section 4 names a section 1 change | Fill "why at risk from this launch" for every row, or remove the row |
| Deflection bought by hiding the contact route | Deflection rises while topic CSAT falls or repeat contacts rise | Keep the counter-metrics beside the target, and count tickets inside the window as not deflected |
| Help content in product vocabulary | Articles answer questions no runbook symptom row asks | Write each piece from a symptom row, and have the support lead review it |
| A feedback loop with no reader | Tickets tagged for weeks, no evidence notes written, nothing reported back | One named reader with a weekly slot, and a dated note to CS on what changed |
