# Failure Scenarios: Tradewind Transfers, UK-to-Pakistan cash payout

Fills [templates/delivery/failure-scenarios.md](../templates/delivery/failure-scenarios.md). Everything here is invented: Tradewind Transfers is a fictional cross-border remittance app sending from the UK and paying out through cash agents in Pakistan, its people are roles filled by invented names, and every number, corridor, agent count and rate is ILLUSTRATIVE, drawn from the remittances domain card and the scenario set this file exists to record rather than from any real money transfer operator, correspondent bank or agent network. See the [examples index](README.md). The corridor model this file assumes is described in the [remittances domain card](../knowledge/domains/remittances.md).

## 1. Scenario table

Every external dependency on this corridor carries at least one row: the sending licence, the receiving-country payout licence, the correspondent and settlement bank, the FX rate feed, sanctions and PEP screening, and the cash agent network itself. The FS-9 row is carried unchanged from the domain card's worked example, because it is the scenario this corridor already lives with.

| ID | Scenario | Blast radius | Detection | Recovery | Data loss risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FS-1 | Correspondent bank serving the UK-to-Pakistan settlement path serves notice on the account and stops clearing for this corridor | Every sender-initiated transfer in the corridor fails to settle; new sends cannot be funded, and in-flight transfers already debited sit unsettled until the fallback bank is live. Senders who already paid cannot be told a date the fallback bank has not confirmed | Notice letter from the correspondent bank; a scheduled SWIFT settlement confirmation going missing two settlement cycles in a row is the earlier signal, alerted by corridor operations | Legal and licensing own the relationship: confirm the notice in writing, engage the two pre-qualified fallback settlement banks, and freeze new corridor sends rather than accept funds that cannot settle; owner: legal and licensing lead (not corridor operations, and not engineering, because this closes the licensed path itself); target 5 business days to a fallback bank live, and new sends paused from the moment notice is confirmed | None: funds already debited are held at the sending entity under the sending licence, not lost |
| FS-2 | FX rate lock expires mid-transfer while the sender's funded transfer is between send and payout | The sender's quoted rate and the rate at payout disagree; anywhere the lock lapses the receiver is offered fewer PKR than the sender was told, and the sender is owed the difference or a re-quote they did not ask for. Blast radius is every transfer funded in the window before the lock lapses | Corridor operations dashboard: rate-lock age against the lock validity window, alerted when any funded transfer is still in flight past the lock expiry | Re-quote at the sender's originally locked rate and absorb the difference where it is inside the corridor's FX margin tolerance, or refund the sender in full if the sender has not yet confirmed payout; owner: corridor operations lead; target 4 hours to clear every transfer caught in the window | None: no transfer record is lost, but the sender's quoted rate and the rate at payout must both be written to the transfer record or a refund cannot be reconciled |
| FS-3 | Sanctions screening returns a false positive on a receiver name, holding the payout | The specific transfer is held as "processing" and the receiver cannot collect at the agent counter; the sender is told the transfer is under review without the screening detail that compliance must not disclose. Blast radius is the held transfer and any transfer matched by the same name pattern until the pattern is cleared | Compliance screening queue: a held transfer appears as a hold event tagged sanctions-review within minutes; the product shows "processing," never "failed," from the moment the hold posts | Compliance reviews the match against the screening source, clears the false positive, and releases the transfer to the agent network; the sender-facing status stays "processing" throughout and never shows a screening reason; owner: compliance duty officer (a compliance decision, not a system bug); target 24 hours to clear a false positive | None: the transfer is held at the sending entity, with the originating and beneficiary information attached per the screening record |
| FS-4 | Receiver name at cash pick-up does not match the beneficiary name on the transfer | The receiver is refused at the agent counter and the transfer cannot be paid out; the funds remain held against the transfer, and the receiver may make a repeat trip before the mismatch is resolved. Blast radius is the affected transfers in the corridor | Agent network reports a name-mismatch refusal from the counter, tagged to the transfer reference; the product shows "processing," never "failed," at the counter and in the app | Corridor operations contacts the sender to confirm the correct beneficiary name and the receiving identity document, corrects the beneficiary record, and re-issues the payout instruction; if the sender cannot confirm, the transfer is refunded to the sender under the sender's refund and cancellation rights; owner: corridor operations lead; target 48 hours to correct or refund | None: the transfer record and the corrected beneficiary name are both written to the audit trail |
| FS-5 | State Bank of Pakistan suspends or narrows the payout licence (or licence exemption) the cash agent network holds to pay out this corridor | Every receiver-side payout in Pakistan stops, regardless of agent liquidity; senders still show "sent," and no rerouting inside the existing agent network resolves it because the network's own authority to pay out is what is suspended | Regulatory notice to the agent network or to Tradewind Transfers directly; corridor operations treats a payout licence renewal or compliance filing going overdue as the earlier signal | Legal and licensing confirm the scope of the suspension, engage a second licensed payout partner already vetted for this corridor, and freeze new corridor sends until a licensed payout path is live; owner: legal and licensing lead (not corridor operations, because this closes the licensed payout path itself, the same class of failure as FS-1 on the sending side); target 10 business days to a licensed fallback payout partner live, longer than FS-1 because a new payout licence relationship takes longer to stand up than a fallback settlement bank | None: funds are held at the sending entity under the sending licence, not lost, but cannot be paid out in Pakistan until a licensed path exists |
| FS-9 | Cash agent network in the PK-Lahore inbound corridor reports zero float at 60% of locations | Every receiver-side payout in that corridor stalls; senders still show "sent," receivers cannot collect | Agent liquidity feed drops below 40% coverage, alerted within 15 minutes | Reroute new payouts to two backup agent networks with live float; hold affected transfers as "processing," never "failed"; owner: corridor operations lead, target 2 hours to reroute | None: funds are held at the sending entity, not lost, but unreachable to the receiver until rerouted |

FS-1 is the scenario the domain card warns about: a correspondent bank can end the relationship that lets the money settle across borders long before any regulator asks the corridor to stop, and the termination is often the true cause of a corridor going dark. Its owner is legal and licensing, not corridor operations and not engineering, because no amount of product work re-opens a closed settlement account. FS-5 is the same class of failure on the other end of the corridor: the domain card names the destination-country payout licence as one of four things that can each fail independently, and a suspension there is no more fixable by rerouting agents than a correspondent-bank exit is fixable by rerouting engineering.

FS-2, FS-3 and FS-4 share one rule stated once here so it holds in every row: a stalled payout shows as "processing," never "failed." A sanctions hold is a compliance decision, and the product must have a way to say "under review" without disclosing what compliance forbids explaining; a name mismatch and a float shortage are likewise recoverable states, not final ones. The reroute owner for FS-9 and FS-4 sits in corridor operations, not engineering, because the recovery is a network action, not a code change.

Regulatory context, as of 2026-09-11, confirm with counsel, not legal advice: where the sender is in the United States, the Remittance Transfer Rule (Regulation E, subpart B, as implemented by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau) gives the sender a right to cancel a remittance transfer and receive a refund, generally within 30 minutes of payment or before the receiver collects the funds if that happens sooner, and error resolution rights after that; where the sender is in the UK or the EU, the applicable payment services rules (the UK's Payment Services Regulations 2017, as amended, and the EU's Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2)) give the sender cancellation and refund rights on an unauthorised or incorrectly executed payment. Sender refund and cancellation handling for FS-2, FS-3 and FS-4 must be built to whichever of these applies to the sender's origin, and the exact timelines must be confirmed with counsel rather than assumed from this file.
