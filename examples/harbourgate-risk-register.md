## 1. Scoring

Likelihood and impact each score 1 to 3, so a score runs 1 to 9 (N68). At 6 or above a row needs an active mitigation with a date, not a watching brief; the register threshold and the 1-to-3 scales are the same ones the security architecture checklist uses, so a finding that clears the checklist's threshold and a risk that clears this one are the same event scored twice, not two different facts. Of the first fourteen rows: R1 to R6 and R10 to R12 opened at the premortem of 2026-04-09; R8 and R13 opened from security findings F1 and F3 that reached the same threshold; R9 and R14 were added 2026-06-16, after rehearsal 1 and HG-INC-14. R7 is dated 2026-06-16 in the identifier table; the migration cutover plan confirms it was raised then, after rehearsal 1 exposed D-019's one-weekend plan, not at the 2026-04-09 premortem. Finding F2, the row that became R14, scored 4 at the STRIDE walk, below the register's threshold of 6, and was routed to backlog item HG-812 instead of this register, which is the gap that produced HG-INC-14. R15 was added 2026-10-16 from the API contract's own open item (section 1): a kiosk authorisation that lands after the kiosk has already shown "pay at the till" is a live double-payment exposure with no interim rule beyond BR-006's 24 h void.

## 5. How this register fails

| Failure mode | What it looked like on this register | The rule that stops it |
|---|---|---|
| No owner | Never happened here; every row in section 2 carries one named owner, not a team | Orphans go to the top of the review, not the bottom |
| Scored once, never revisited | R3 sat at 4 from the premortem until rehearsal 1 proved the score wrong; nobody had re-scored it in the eight weeks DEP-2 sat unresolved | Re-score at every review; a risk row untouched for a month is flagged by the register owner at the weekly review. A6 escalates any dependency stuck at "requested" for more than 4 weeks, which catches this case, but A6 checks dependencies, not risk rows directly |
| Mitigation restates the risk | Never happened here; the example row's placeholder ("prevent the outage") was deleted before any real row was written | A mitigation names an action, an owner and a date, or it is not one |
| Everything is medium | R1 was scored a medium (4, L2 x I2) at the premortem, raised by an engineer, and it occurred anyway on 2026-05-19: the register's job was to make the reversal cheap, and it did, because the options D-017 chose from had already been written down when the risk was raised. Nine rows opened at the 2026-04-09 premortem scored 3 to 6 | Force a spread. A long amber list with no triage is itself a finding |
| Closed risks deleted | Never happened here; R1 to R14 all remain visible in section 2 or section 4 | Closed rows are archived in section 4, not removed |
| Accept without the signature | R4 sat in section 2 as an accepted risk with nobody named for four and a half months (2026-04-09 to 2026-08-24), including through Gate 3, Gate 5 attempts 2 and 3, and the Marlowe and Tidewater cohorts, contrary to ADR-0003's condition that an acceptor be named before the first provider moved, until D-022 added the signature in section 3 | Every accept response, at any score, gets a name and a date in section 3 within the review cycle it is proposed, not whenever the diary allows |
| The register replaces escalation | This is exactly what happened to R14: finding F2 scored 4, below the register's threshold of 6, was logged, and was routed to a backlog nobody scheduled instead of this register or an escalation. HG-INC-14 is the cost of that gap | A finding routed anywhere other than this register carries a named owner and a scheduled date; one with no date by the next weekly review becomes a row here at its own score. Above the threshold, a row also names who was told and when: F1 and F3 followed the rule and became R8 and R13 without incident |
