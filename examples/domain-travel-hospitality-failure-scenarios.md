Fills [templates/delivery/failure-scenarios.md](../templates/delivery/failure-scenarios.md). An ILLUSTRATIVE partner-hotel scenario sits beside the airline rows, because the travel card is explicit that this product is an orchestration layer over somebody else's inventory: FS-6, a partner hotel walks 9 guests holding a confirmed Larkhollow package booking on the 2026-10-18 stay because the property is oversold. The hotel, not Larkhollow, is the supplier, but Larkhollow combined the flight and the room into a package, so [the EU Package Travel Directive (Directive (EU) 2015/2302)](../knowledge/domains/travel-hospitality.md) organizer-liability and insolvency-protection duties are in play, and the assessment of whether the walking is an extraordinary circumstance, or the hotel's ordinary overbooking, is made before any compensation decision. Detection is the hotel's own walk notice plus the agent escalation; recovery is a same-night alternative room at the hotel's cost, a package-price refund where the guest declines, and the package organizer's statutory duty to ensure the guest is not stranded; owner: Priyanka Iyer, Disruption Ops; target: an alternative room offered within 2 hours of the walk notice, all 9 guests placed before midnight. Data loss risk: none, but a guest not placed before midnight converts into a chargeback plus a package-liability exposure. This row carries the card's organizer-liability reading directly, and is the reason the product does not combine flight and room into a package without checking the eligibility rules first.

FS-4 and FS-6 have no rehearsal date at the time of this register, which is the honest gap the exit gate below records.
- The scenario we most doubt our recovery steps for: FS-5, mass rebooking. The December 2022 Southwest meltdown's root cause was crew scheduling, not rebooking, but the passenger-facing symptom, a rebooking backlog customers could not get through, is exactly the failure mode FS-5's queue-depth path has to survive, and we have not yet proved our batch mode absorbs that volume with the re-accommodation offers going out inside the 4-hour target, not merely the cancellations being processed.

## How this failure-scenarios register fails

| Failure mode | What it looks like | The rule that stops it |
|---|---|---|
| Detection is the customer | The row says "a customer reports it", and the fix is a support macro | Machine detection on every row, or an explicit section 2 gap with an owner and a date |
| Recovery is steps with no clock | "Contact the passenger" with no time estimate, and no name | Every recovery row names an owner and a time estimate, because the compensation is owed on a regulator's clock |
| Compensation is treated as a support ticket | The passenger has to file a claim to be paid | The card's own worked example rule: a recovery step that runs before the customer files anything, because the obligation is on a regulator's clock, not the support queue's |
| One regime assumed global | The EU261 tier is applied to a US-departing flight, or the US DOT refund is applied to a European one | Each row names the regime and the regulator it answers to, and the EU and US regimes do not defer to each other |
| Extraordinary circumstances assumed | The carve-out is applied automatically to any cancellation, so no refund is offered | A documented human assessment before any compensation decision, with the outcome and its basis stated to the passenger |
| The exception path is unmonitored | The booking engine is instrumented and the disruption path is not | Section 2's gaps are written as change requests against the observability card, and the exception path is instrumented first |
