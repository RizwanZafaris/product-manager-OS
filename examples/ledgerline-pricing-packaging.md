## 2. Pricing model

- **Model (Killed 2026-12-18 per EXP-1, N62; ratified D6 2026-12-21; held only for the 73 paid accounts, N93; do not quote):** Subscription add-on, billed monthly per plan seat, layered on top of the existing Business-plan subscription. Not usage-based, not freemium, not a one-time fee.
- **Why this model for this buyer:** Business-plan buyers pay a flat $149 per account per month (N27), though every account carries a seat count (N34); the nearest competitive alternative, Cinderwick, sells per seat at $8 a month (N13). Pricing the add-on per seat on top of a flat plan is what produced the $204-a-month bill for the median account (N47), against the $149 the account already pays. Billing already held a seat count and sales already forecast in seats, but the add-on's own seat meter still had to be built (DEP1, 1.5 person-months, N86, delivered 2026-11-19), chosen over a usage meter for drafted reports (DEP4, 1 person-month, N87) that did not exist at signing; ADR-007, which formalized the seat-based charge, followed on 2026-11-04, the day after this document was signed. The model that could ship for phase 1 on 2026-11-10 (N43) was the seat model, not the model the evidence most favoured.
- **Failure mode accepted:** Seat pricing bills for people who hold a plan seat but rarely file, so an account's price scales with headcount rather than with the reports the copilot drafts. Per the [B2B SaaS card](../knowledge/domains/saas-b2b.md), what drives expansion, seats, usage or tier upgrades, is supposed to be a decision, not an accident; this is the accident. The honest reason for the choice was what billing could already do, not what the evidence favoured. Building the seat meter itself cost about 1.5 person-months, about $15,000 at the $10,000-a-person-month engineering rate (N12, N86), and does not carry over to a usage price if one ships.

Every drafted report also carries a model API cost, about $0.92 per drafted report at the quoted rate (N9 to N11); this is not fully loaded, since it excludes the hosting and on-call share (N8) the AI products card's fully loaded definition also counts, and the quote is dated to the business case, 2026-08-13, not contracted (DEP2). At the median S-1 account, the seat price carries revenue of 34 seats x $6 = $204 a month (N47) against model cost of about $10 a month at the mean (11 reports a month x $0.92 = $10.12) and about $6.44 a month at the median (7 reports x $0.92) (N33, N11): about 95% margin on the model line at either reading, so the 60% gross-margin floor (N49) is not a live risk at this price. Per the [AI products card](../knowledge/domains/ai-products.md), a margin built on today's token price needs a dated assumption, which is the 2026-08-13 quote above; the tight margin belongs instead to the usage price the growth plan proposes, about 62% (N89), where R6 tracks the exposure if the volume re-quote (DEP2) comes back worse.

**Rejected: free bundle (D2).** Bundling the copilot at no charge into the Business plan was argued twice, by sales at the positioning session on 2026-10-30 and by a design partner at this pricing review, and lost both times on margin. At the business case's own 60% adoption assumption, a free bundle across the 2,900 S-1 accounts drafts about 229,680 reports a year, about 965,000 receipts at 4.2 receipts a report (N10), about $212,000 a year in model cost at the $0.22 quoted rate (N9), about 1.9 points of the 78% Business-plan gross margin (N28, N51). At 100% adoption the cost is about $354,000 a year, about 3.1 points (N52). Even a friendlier re-quote does not flip the arithmetic: at an assumed $0.14 a receipt, the volume DEP2 is chasing, a free bundle still costs about $135,000 a year, about 1.2 points (N53). Daniel Okafor's rule, recorded at D2: no feature costing more than one point of Business-plan gross margin ships without its own revenue line (N50). A ten-report free allowance was rejected too, on the grounds that the accounts that need the product most are exactly the accounts that would exhaust it.

What this table never carried, at signing on 2026-11-03 or at the 2026-12-21 reopening, is a row for the account's own Business-plan price. A median S-1 account runs 34 seats (N34); at $6 a seat the add-on costs $204 a month (N47), against a $149-a-month Business plan (N27), about 137% of the plan itself. Nobody benchmarked the add-on against the product it was bolted onto, only against the competitor and the free alternative. The positioning document's section 5, signed 2026-10-30, set this bar in writing before this price existed: the category was expected to make the buyer assume "the price is a small fraction of the flat $149 account price," and its third row pointed at the vendor anchor ($8 a seat, N13), which for the median 34-seat account works out to $272, above the $149 plan (N13, N34), so that whatever unit and number pricing chose, it had to clear that bar or break the promise. That bar was written down and not read when the price was set. The win-loss review found the same gap later in the buyer's own words (N77): 4 of 6 interviews named the seat-priced total as an obstacle, seats billed for people who never file, a total above the plan, or a total above the buyer's own budget. WL-06 (Ashgrove Property Management, lost to Cinderwick) is logged there as an accepted loss type, not chased. The win-loss review recommends to Isabel Ferreira that Cinderwick's quoted rate ($8 per seat for 900 seats, N13) stay on record here as a competitive benchmark, not a floor, regardless of the packaging pivot. That row is not added here as an after-the-fact fix, because the point this document records is that it was missing when the price was set.

## 6. Review

- **Review cadence:** Every two quarters, and at each Gate 6 review of the add-on. The OKR cycle running 2026-11-02 to 2026-12-31 (N69), scored 2026-12-21, is the first Gate 6 touchpoint this cadence aligns to.
- **What triggers an off-cycle review:** A win-loss pattern naming the value metric, a competitor price move, or the EXP-1 experiment's own pre-declared kill rule (N57). Two of those three fired together here: R5 was realised on 2026-12-18 (N61), 4 of 6 win-loss interviews named the seat-priced total as an obstacle, unprompted (N77), and the advisory board said the same thing 16 days before the kill, on 2026-12-02, with 6 of 8 members present agreeing (N78, EV-AB01).
- **Next review:** Open: Isabel Ferreira owns the date. It is not scheduled ahead of EXP-2's own kill-or-ship date of 2027-02-12 (N92); section 1 stays open until that result is known.

A price change worth testing before committing runs as a pricing experiment through [experiment-brief.md](../templates/operate/experiment-brief.md), decision rule and all, before it lands in section 2. That order was not followed here: the $6 seat price was already signed into this section 2 on 2026-11-03, before any test ran. EXP-1, the per-seat offer anchored on the reviewer's hourly cost, ran between 2026-11-23 and 2026-12-04 (N54 to N62) and tested the presentation of that already signed price, not the price or its unit, and is the reason section 1 is reopened. EXP-2 is the first test this document runs before its price lands: the usage re-offer at $2.40 per drafted report (N89) is queued to become the next section 2 entry only once the usage meter ships (D7, DEP4) and the test itself concludes, and this document is due to be re-signed once EXP-2's own kill-or-ship date resolves.
