## 1. Strategic context: the diagnosis

- **The situation:** The copilot is proven on the product, not on the price. In the add-on window 2026-11-23 to 2026-12-18, 76% of customer drafted reports were approved first time, against S-1's measured median of 66% before the copilot existed (N65, N35), and 47% of eligible reports in active accounts were drafted (N65), while the per-seat offer converted 2.9% in the anchored arm and 2.4% in the plain arm against a pre-declared kill line of 4.0% in both arms (N61, N57, N62). The buyer's objection was not the feature: four of six win-loss accounts named the seat-priced total itself (N77), six of eight advisory-board members named seat pricing as penalising accounts with few filers sixteen days before the kill (N78), and the median account's $204 a month sat above the $149 plan it was an add-on to (N47, N27). The crux was already visible in the pricing review's own weighted matrix: the metric that tracks delivered value scored a 1 against seats' 5 (LC11).
- **The crux:** The value metric. Seats bill for people who never file and price above the plan for exactly the accounts the copilot serves best (N34, N47, N77), and the north star counts drafted reports approved, so any price that grows by headcount rather than by drafted reports is structurally mispriced (M-001; LC11; D3 accepted the mismatch in writing on 2026-11-03).
- **What changed recently:** Last year's strategy was "build the internal copilot and earn its keep on time savings", funded by the business case alone and closed at internal Gate 6 PERSIST (D4, 2026-11-09). The commercial pass started on 2026-10-15 only when the Q3 win-loss batch showed 9 of 31 lost Business-plan deals naming receipt capture and 7 of those nine choosing Cinderwick (N37, D1), and it ended on 2026-12-21 when the experiment's own kill rule executed the PIVOT on packaging (N62, D6). An internal-tool strategy cannot be carried into a paid add-on unchanged, because at customer tenancy model calls are cost of goods sold the internal build only absorbed: the free-bundle arithmetic at the 60% adoption assumption costs about $212,000 a year against a 78%-margin plan, about 1.9 points of gross margin (N51, N28), against Daniel Okafor's one-point rule (N50, D2).

## 1b. The guiding policy

**In one sentence:** Charge every account only for drafted reports it actually produces, refuse any billing unit that grows faster than the value the account receives, and keep the copilot as a priced add-on that never rides the $149 plan's margin for free.

| Test | Answer |
|---|---|
| The opposite policy, written out | Bundle the copilot free into the Business plan to win the Cinderwick deals outright, and keep seats, plan headcount or flat per-account fees as the billing unit because billing already holds the seat count. Isabel Ferreira, Ruth Adeyemi and one design partner argued exactly this and lost, so it is a live alternative rather than a straw man (D2, 2026-10-30 and 2026-11-03; D3, 2026-11-03; N51 to N53 against N50; N93) |
| What this policy refuses | The fastest route to the lost Cinderwick deals: a free or flat feature that wins accounts at $149 without a margin line of its own, and any price, including a discount, that papers over a unit mismatch instead of fixing it (N50, N51; D6 rejected "persist with discounts" on 2026-12-21 because a discount cannot correct a value-metric mismatch; D7 rejected $1.60 per drafted report at $2.40's same bar because $1.60 sits under the margin floor at the quoted cost, LC51's 0.92 / 0.40 = $2.30) |
| Who has to change what they do on Monday | Priya Nair's team builds the drafted-report usage meter instead of seat metering: the 1.5 person-months and $15,000 already spent on seats (DEP1, ADR-007, N86) are not reused, ADR-008 supersedes it for the add-on, and 1 person-month, $10,000, is needed by 2027-01-08 (DEP4, N87). ## 3. How we win: differentiation

- **Our edge:** The account's own written policy line, surfaced beside each drafted field inside the approval flow the account already runs, so a bounce becomes a conversation with a rule before submission rather than a rejection after it.
- **Why it holds:** A position, not a feature. A stand-alone capture vendor sees receipts but not the account's approval history or its policy text, and a free bundle is not an edge, it is the announcement that the value is zero (D2's second reason, with the N51 margin arithmetic beside it). The audit says the position is a candidate barrier rather than a held one today (LC9), and the mechanism that converts candidate to barrier is named rather than hoped for: the approval-rate tile (LEDGERLINE-S5), which makes the platform's own approval data visible inside the workflow. Honest caveat, in the audit's own words: none of switching costs, counter-positioning, scale, network, branding or process is held at 2026-10-28 (LC9), and extraction accuracy on German-language receipts sits at 71% against the 90% threshold (N83, N20, R3), so the edge does not currently extend to accounts filing in other languages.
- **The named alternative it beats:** Typing receipt data into the Expenses form and, for the accounts that distrust that, a shadow spreadsheet, a tool four of six interviewed design partners keep (N40, EV-C01 to EV-C06), plus a trialled or bought stand-alone capture vendor such as Cinderwick (EV-C02, EV-C03; N37, N13, quoted 2026-08-13 at $8 per seat per month for 900 seats).

### Worked micro-example (ILLUSTRATIVE, invented)

*Diagnosis: reps do not avoid filing expenses because the form is slow; they avoid it because a rejected claim costs them a second submission and an argument. Filing time is a symptom.*

*Guiding policy: optimise for first-time acceptance, not for filing speed. We will refuse work that shortens the form at the cost of accuracy.*

*Coherent action: extraction accuracy on the fields finance rejects most, an in-app pre-check against policy before submission, and no autofill of any field we cannot verify. We are explicitly not building bulk upload, which is the most requested feature and does not touch acceptance.*

*What would kill it: acceptance rate does not move while filing time does. Checked at the end of the pilot.*
