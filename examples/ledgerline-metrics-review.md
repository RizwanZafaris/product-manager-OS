- The most surprising thing in the data this window: how cleanly the miss separated. Every product-quality input, M-004 through M-006, landed within a few points of its Phase 1 or internal figure. Only the one input tied to the price, M-007, missed by more than half. A launch this clean on the product side and this bad on conversion is not the usual shape of a bad launch.
- What we will stop doing, based on the above: pricing the add-on by a unit nobody on the buying side reads as the value delivered. Four of six win-loss interviews (N77) and 6 of 8 advisory board members, session on 2026-12-02 (N78, EV-AB01), named the same thing before the kill: seats do not track filers, and a $204-a-month median charge (N47) reads as expensive against a $149 plan regardless of anchor copy. The advisory board met first, on 2026-12-02; the win-loss interviews ran 2026-12-07 to 2026-12-16 and were signed 2026-12-17; both landed before the 2026-12-18 kill, not one before it and one after.

## 5. Decision

- **Decision:** PIVOT
  - The problem this add-on targets, bounced first-submission reports at accounts like the ones in segment S-5, is still real and still unsolved for most of the 1,240 S-5 accounts (N36); the data sheet does not record how many of the 79 active accounts (N64) sit within S-5. The drafting and approval mechanism that would solve it held, short of its committed targets: KR2 scored 0.83 (76% against a 78% target) and KR3 scored 0.94 (47% against a 50% target), both committed key results, not aspirational ones. What is not working is the price: plan seats as the value metric. Approval on drafted reports held at 76% while conversion failed, so the product worked and the price did not; that is the diagnosis this review turns on.
- Decider: Isabel Ferreira, Chief Product Officer · Date: 2026-12-21
- What changes: D6 kills the per-seat price per the EXP-1 pre-declared rule (N57, N61, N62), pivots packaging to a usage metric, holds Phase 3 of the GTM plan at its unmet entry condition, swaps the offer page to the alternate wording drafted 2026-11-06 (N94), reopens [pricing and packaging](ledgerline-pricing-packaging.md) section 1, and withdraws the pricing section of the sales enablement one-pager: six consequences already in motion. Maya Chen is commissioned to bring the next bet, a usage price with no seat charge held to the same 6.0% bar the seat price failed, to a growth plan by 2026-12-22; that plan, once signed, carries the D7 decision and the EXP-2 design.
  - **Options argued and rejected:** iterating the anchor copy was rejected because both arms sat under the 4.0% kill line, leaving no live arm to iterate on (N57); persisting with discounts was rejected because N48 forbids any discount beyond the two already agreed without Isabel Ferreira and a decision-log entry, and a discount does not fix a value-metric mismatch. PERSIST was not chosen because the one input tied to the price, M-007, missed the ship bar by more than half while the product mechanism held (section 4 above). SUNSET was not chosen because the trigger evidence (N37, N38) and the S-5 segment it targets were not invalidated, only the value metric was.
  - This is a deviation from the template's default, which sends a pivot back to DISCOVER with a new discovery document; the growth plan substitutes for it here. No buyer has yet been asked any per-report price directly: 0 of 6 design-partner interviewees were asked any price (N40), and the win-loss buyers (N76) saw only the per-seat price, and 5 of 8 advisory board members asked for per-report pricing without a number ever being put to them (N78). The next experiment, owned by Maya Chen, is the instrument that will put a number to a buyer for the first time; it does not stand in for a discovery pass, and this review names that gap rather than papering over it.
  - Named costs, all ILLUSTRATIVE: two Enterprise quotes withdrawn and 73 paid accounts holding the old price for 12 months (N88, N93); a 27-day DPA slip that held those two Enterprise quotes until D5 (DEP3, N79); 1.5 person-months ($15,000) of seat-metering engineering that the usage model will not reuse (N86); 1 person-month ($10,000) for the usage meter still needed before the next experiment can run (N87). These are costs of the about 7 weeks this took (N88), not costs this review hides in a footnote.
