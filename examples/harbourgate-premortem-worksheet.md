Fills [frameworks/execution/premortem-worksheet.md](../frameworks/execution/premortem-worksheet.md). Everything here is invented: Harbourgate, Quay, Kestrel, Marlowe and Tidewater are fictional, every person is fictional, and every number, date, rate and pound is ILLUSTRATIVE, chosen so that this worksheet reconciles with the [Harbourgate journey](harbourgate-journey.md) and the [coverage sheet](harbourgate-coverage-sheet.md), never to be quoted as a benchmark or copied as a target.

**Owner:** Ife Adeyemi, Product Manager · **Facilitator:** Noor Haddad · **Session date:** 2026-04-09 · **Status:** Filled DESIGN worksheet before Gate 3 · **Product:** Harbourgate checkout modernisation

Based on the ideas of Gary Klein, from "Performing a Project Premortem", Harvard Business Review (2007). Explained here in this repository's own words.

## What it is for

Prospective hindsight. The room was told that Harbourgate's Quay migration had already failed on 2026-10-09. Participants then produced specific causes without debating whether each cause was likely. The worksheet captures the failure headline, causes by category, votes, arithmetic, mitigations and owners.

The decision improved by this session was which risks needed owners before the plan was signed. The session made visible that R1, shadow-comparison retries authorising the same card twice, received one likelihood vote and no mitigation row. This is the seed's "not weighted heavily enough" made visible in the record.

## Run it when

- Before Gate 3, as the risk half of "architecture and risks reviewed"
- Before the Harbourgate migration plan was signed
- Before a cutover involving three payment providers and an external dependency
- When the payment migration appeared to have a workable design but the room needed to test whether the risk register was complete

**Skip it when:** the plan does not exist yet. Harbourgate had a plan, architecture decisions and a risk register before this session.

### Step 3: votes

Each of the seven voters had three likelihood votes and one unrecoverable vote. Noor Haddad facilitated and did not vote. Saoirse Whelan was the outsider. The score arithmetic was:

- Likelihood votes: `5 + 0 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 21`
- Unrecoverable votes: `1 + 3 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 = 7`
- Votes available: `7 voters x 3 likelihood votes = 21`
- Unrecoverable votes available: `7 voters x 1 unrecoverable vote = 7`
- Score formula: `likelihood votes + (2 x unrecoverable votes)`

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| R2 | 5 | 1 | `5 + (2 x 1) = 7` |
| R4 | 0 | 3 | `0 + (2 x 3) = 6` |
| R5 | 3 | 1 | `3 + (2 x 1) = 5` |
| R7 | 2 | 1 | `2 + (2 x 1) = 4` |
| R10 | 2 | 1 | `2 + (2 x 1) = 4` |

Every row above matches HC36 exactly. R1's single likelihood vote and zero unrecoverable votes explain why it did not cross the mitigation threshold, even though it later occurred. It was not weighted heavily enough in this session.

## Reading the result

The top cause was R2, with a score of 7. It was already on the register but did not yet have a named owner, so the mitigation made the existing risk actionable.

R4 scored 6 despite receiving no likelihood votes because it received three unrecoverable votes:

`0 + (2 x 3) = 6`

It therefore received an owner and an acceptance path. This is the same logic as treating a severe impact as requiring action even when likelihood votes are low.

The causes were concentrated in delivery:

- Delivery causes: `R2, R3, R5, R6, R10, R12 = 6`
- Total causes: `10`
- Delivery share: `6 / 10 = 60%`

This concentration showed that the session was testing the migration mechanics more heavily than value, usability or security.

R1 is the important miss. This session's cause row opened register row R1 directly, but the premortem assigned it only one likelihood vote, no unrecoverable vote and a score of 1. The risk later occurred on 2026-05-19, and D-017 reversed the shadow-comparison approach on 2026-05-21. The worksheet therefore records both truths: the risk was present, and the room did not weight it heavily enough.

R7 was a cause in this session, with a score of 4 (two likelihood votes, one unrecoverable vote); its register score of 9 was not assigned until 2026-06-16, after rehearsal 1, as reconciliation note 1 of the [coverage sheet](harbourgate-coverage-sheet.md) records. R9 and R14 were never imagined here at all. Both were found later through delivery evidence, not retroactively inserted into this session.

R8 and R13 are excluded from the cause table because they came from F1 and F3. Their presence in the register on the premortem date does not make them causes generated by this worksheet.

## ILLUSTRATIVE example

This is the filled Harbourgate example, not a second invented session. The session used seven voters, Noor Haddad as facilitator, Saoirse Whelan as the outsider, the 2026-10-09 failure date and the HC36 vote table.

The most important teaching point is the mismatch between the visible risk and its weighting:

- R1 was raised by an engineer.
- R1 received `1` likelihood vote.
- R1 received `0` unrecoverable votes.
- R1 scored `1 + (2 x 0) = 1`.
- R1 received no mitigation row.
- R1 occurred on 2026-05-19.
- D-017 reversed the shadow-comparison approach on 2026-05-21.

The page does not rewrite the result after the fact. It keeps the low vote, the absent mitigation and the later outcome together.

## The trap

The trap was treating the session as a brainstorm rather than a prospective failure exercise. The Harbourgate record avoids that in four ways:

- Causes are written as events, not labels such as "integration risk".
- Votes are shown as arithmetic, including the unrecoverable weighting.
- R1 remains visible without a retroactive mitigation row.
- R9 and R14 remain absent because they were never imagined in the session.

The record also avoids changing the instrument. Register scores use likelihood and impact on 1 to 3 scales, as recorded by N68. Premortem scores use likelihood votes plus twice the unrecoverable votes. These are different instruments and are not restated as one another.
