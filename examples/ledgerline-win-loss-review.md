## 2. What we heard

- Primary reason for the outcome: the add-on bills by plan seat, and only 9 of Redfern's 48 seats belong to anyone who files an expense report, so the price attached to a cost that most of the seats never generate
- Secondary reasons: the anchor on the offer page compared the price to a reviewer's monthly hours, and Redfern's reviewer is one person, not a team; no one internally owned "evaluate a new finance tool" as a task with a deadline
- The job they were hiring for, in their words: "get the nine people who actually turn in receipts through approval without me chasing them, without adding a bill for the other thirty-nine who never will"
- What the account admin or system of record logged before this interview: not recorded. No CRM entry, rep note, or sales-ops code for Redfern's non-decision is on the data sheet, only the platform's own funnel and billing events (N60, N76). ## 3. What they chose instead, or as well

- Alternative chosen: nothing; Redfern kept typing receipt data into the Expenses form, the same workaround named in the positioning document's first alternative
- Why it fit their job better: it cost nothing and the nine actual filers already knew it; a $288 monthly add-on, $234 of it for thirty-nine seats that never file, read as paying for people, not for the job
- What would have changed the outcome, per the buyer (speculative): a price tied to the nine people who file, not the forty-eight who could, a per-filer shape, not per-report; the finance lead did not name a metric or use the word usage, and per-active-filer pricing was already rejected at D3 for having no agreed definition (also asked for explicitly, as per-filer or per-report, by WL-04 in section 4)
