## What it is for

This worksheet tests whether Ledgerline's customer-facing approval-rate tile can create a closed acquisition loop. The candidate mechanism is narrow: an account admin sees the account's approval-rate tile, LEDGERLINE-S5, and shows it to a peer Business account. That peer might then arrive at the add-on offer and become an active account.

The observed data does not establish a closed loop. Among the 79 active add-on accounts, referral arrivals were 0 of 79, and no referral event was instrumented (LC6). The honest output is therefore a single-touch funnel, not a compounding growth loop. The worksheet still records the candidate mechanism so the missing instrumentation and the funnel leak are explicit.

The method is based on the ideas of Brian Balfour and Casey Winters, from the Reforge growth essays and programs (2018 onward), explained here in the repository's own words.

## Run it when

- A product has a possible spillover through account admins, peers, shared output or approval data.
- The growth plan needs to distinguish a self-reinforcing acquisition mechanism from a linear offer funnel.
- A team is considering whether an in-product proof point, such as LEDGERLINE-S5, creates new account arrivals.
- The team needs to locate the step that limits the next experiment.

**Skip it when:** no path exists from one account's use to another account's arrival without a salesperson or a new exposure. In this case, the candidate path is recorded, but the measured evidence does not yet show a loop.

## The worksheet

### Step 1: draw the loop

The candidate loop is recorded without treating funnel rates as loop rates. The last row is intended to feed the first row, but the output has not been observed.

| Step | Input (what enters) | Action (who does what) | Output (what comes out) | Output feeds step | Rate r (output / input) | Users generated per action (g) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Active add-on account with an account admin | The admin opens the approval-rate tile, LEDGERLINE-S5 | Admin who can show the tile to a peer Business account | 2 | Not measured | Not measured |
| 2 | Admin with the approval-rate tile | The admin shows LEDGERLINE-S5 to a peer Business account | Peer Business account that may arrive through a referral | 3 | Not measurable from current events; observed referral arrivals are 0 of 79 active accounts | Not measured |
| 3 | Peer Business account that may have arrived through a referral | The peer opens the offer, reaches the price step, and may pay | Active add-on account that could return to step 1 | 1 | Not measurable as a loop rate; EXP-1 records funnel events, not referral-linked loop events | Not measured |

The only observed referral arithmetic is:

- Referral arrivals / active add-on accounts = 0 / 79 = 0%.
- The denominator is the 79 active accounts in N64.
- No referral event was instrumented, so 0% is an observed count of identified referral arrivals, not proof that no account heard about the offer from another account.

The EXP-1 funnel rates are kept separate from the candidate loop:

- Exposed to opened = 1,044 / 2,748 = 38% (LC6, from N59 and N60).
- Opened to price step = 302 / 1,044 = 29% (LC6).
- Price step to paid = 73 / 302 = 24% (LC6).

### Step 2: loop math

| Quantity | Symbol | Formula | Value | How measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starting cohort | N0 | activated users from external acquisition per period | Not applicable to this candidate loop | EXP-1 has 2,748 exposed accounts, but that is an assigned exposure cohort, not a cohort generated by this candidate loop |
| Reinvestment factor | k | r1 x r2 x ... x rn x g | Not measurable | r1, r2, r3 and g are not instrumented as referral-linked events. The observed referral count is 0 / 79, but it cannot substitute for the missing loop rates |
| Cycle time | t | median days from a user's activation to the activation of a user they generated | Not available | No referral arrival event or linked activation time is instrumented |
| Users from one cycle | N1 | N0 x k | Not available x not measurable = not computable | Neither N0 for the loop nor k is available |
| Long-run amplification (k under 1) | A | 1 / (1 minus k) | Not computable | k is not measurable |
| Cycles per quarter | c | 90 / t | Not computable | t is not available |
| Users in a quarter |  | N0 x (1 + k + k^2 + ... + k^c) | Not computable | N0, k and c are not available |

The available arithmetic describes the funnel, not loop growth:

- 1,044 of 2,748 exposed accounts opened the offer: 1,044 / 2,748 = 38%.
- 302 of 1,044 opened accounts reached the price step: 302 / 1,044 = 29%.
- 73 of 302 accounts at the price step paid: 73 / 302 = 24%.
- 73 paid accounts plus 6 design partners produced 79 active add-on accounts: 73 + 6 = 79.

The 24% price-step-to-paid rate is the clearest conversion leak in the measured path. The 29% opened-to-price-step rate is also a drop, but the growth-plan diagnosis treats the add-on as a single-touch funnel with the leak at the price step because payment after price exposure is the decision point that failed the price hypothesis.

### Step 3: loop or funnel

| Question | Loop | Funnel |
|---|---|---|
| Does one cycle's output re-enter without new spend? |  | **No measured evidence.** The candidate output is a peer account, but identified referral arrivals are 0 of 79 and no referral event is instrumented. |
| Can k be measured from instrumented events? |  | **Not yet.** The candidate loop has no referral-linked rates or users-generated-per-action value. |
| Is t shorter than the planning period? |  | **Not yet.** No referral-to-activation cycle time exists. |
| Are activation and retention leaks already fixed? |  | **Fix the price leak first.** The measured price-step-to-paid rate is 73 / 302 = 24%, and the observed active-account result is 79 against the growth plan's 180 target in N70. |

**Decision:** use the funnel column. The Ledgerline add-on is a single-touch funnel for this decision, not a growth loop. Do not use a value for k, A, cycle time or quarterly loop amplification in the growth plan.

## Reading the result

The candidate loop has one plausible product path, LEDGERLINE-S5, but no measured reinvestment. The 0 of 79 referral-arrival count and the absence of an instrumented referral event mean that k cannot be calculated. A zero observed referral count must not be presented as a measured loop rate because the event is missing.

The measured funnel is:

1. 2,748 accounts were exposed.
2. 1,044 opened the offer, and 1,044 / 2,748 = 38%.
3. 302 reached the price step, and 302 / 1,044 = 29%.
4. 73 paid, and 73 / 302 = 24%.
5. 73 paid accounts plus 6 design partners resulted in 79 active accounts.

The growth-plan implication is to fix the price step rather than claim compounding acquisition. The candidate loop can be revisited only after referral exposure, referral arrival and referral-linked activation are instrumented. Until then, the step to push is the measured price step in the funnel, using the usage re-offer described in the growth plan.

## ILLUSTRATIVE example

Ledgerline's candidate mechanism is an account-admin proof-point funnel:

| Step | Input | Action | Output | Rate | g |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Active add-on account | Admin opens LEDGERLINE-S5, the approval-rate tile | Admin with a proof point to share | Not measured | Not measured |
| 2 | Admin with the tile | Admin shows the tile to a peer Business account | Potential referred account | 0 / 79 = 0% identified referral arrivals, with no referral event instrumented | Not measured |
| 3 | Potential referred account | Account opens the offer, reaches the price step and may pay | Active add-on account | 73 / 302 = 24% from price step to paid in EXP-1 | Not a loop rate |

The funnel arithmetic is:

- 1,044 / 2,748 = 38% exposed to opened.
- 302 / 1,044 = 29% opened to price step.
- 73 / 302 = 24% price step to paid.
- 73 + 6 = 79 active add-on accounts.

A loop calculation would require values for r1, r2, r3 and g:

- k = r1 x r2 x r3 x g.
- k = not measurable because the referral-linked rates and g are not available.
- A = 1 / (1 minus k) = not computable.
- t = not available, so c = 90 / t is also not computable.

The correct decision is therefore funnel, with the leak at the price step. The growth plan should not describe LEDGERLINE-S5 as a compounding acquisition loop.

## The trap

The trap is converting a product share action into a loop claim. An admin may be able to show the approval-rate tile to a peer, but the data does not show that this action generated a referred arrival. The count of 0 of 79 is bounded by missing instrumentation, not proof that the tile has no influence.

The opposite trap is to use the EXP-1 funnel rates as r1, r2 and r3. Those rates describe assigned offer exposure, offer opening, price-step reach and payment:

- 1,044 / 2,748 = 38%.
- 302 / 1,044 = 29%.
- 73 / 302 = 24%.

They do not identify which account generated which other account. Multiplying them would produce a funnel conversion calculation, not k.

The growth decision is consequently conservative: treat the add-on as a single-touch funnel, fix the price-step leak, and add referral instrumentation before rerunning the loop worksheet.
