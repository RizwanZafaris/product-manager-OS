# Good-Better-Best Packaging: Expense Copilot, post-pivot tiers

Fills [frameworks/pricing/packaging-good-better-best.md](../frameworks/pricing/packaging-good-better-best.md). Everything here is invented: Ledgerline is a fictional mid-market software company, the copilot is the fictional product used across this repository, and every tier, fence, price, date and count is ILLUSTRATIVE, built from the journey's pivot decisions and the coverage sheet's survey so the sheet can be checked against the data it is supposed to draw from. Nothing here is a benchmark or a target to copy. See the [examples index](README.md).

**Owner:** Maya Chen, Product Manager (P1) · **Pricing owner:** Isabel Ferreira (P4) · **Date:** 2027-01-14

## What it is for

The per-seat price died on 2026-12-18 under EXP-1's own rule (N62) and the metrics review pivoted packaging to a usage metric on 2026-12-21 (D6). EXP-2 tests the usage metric as one flat price, $2.40 per drafted report (D7, N89). One flat price answers "does the metric convert"; it does not answer "at what price, for whom, with what included", which is the job this sheet does for the successor price the 73 held accounts move to (N93, R8). The anchor is the Ledgerline product itself, the Business plan at $149 per account per month (N27), because that is the line the buyer already budgets in and the line the add-on sits beside.

## Run it when

- The pivot produced a second segment with a different willingness to pay: EV-S01's single panel put the acceptable range at roughly $1.80 to $2.60 per drafted report with $2.40 near the expensive edge (LC48), and LC49's rungs at $2.00 and $2.40, whose revenue index (LC50) sits within 5% of the maximum, which is the ladder saying one rung does not span the whole range.
- A capability needs a home that is not "everyone gets it": the 11 legacy accounts using the audit export, and the Enterprise prospects' SSO requirement, both sit above the flat usage price's envelope.
- The 73 held accounts' 12-month price holds expire from late November 2027 (N93, LC33) and need a named successor tier, not a re-quote in the dark.

### 2. Tiers

| Tier | Segment | Included | Fences at the boundary above | Price (from the ladder) | Upgrade trigger | Cannibalization risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Good | the 1,660 S-1 accounts outside the best-fit segment (derived: 2,900 minus 1,240, LC3), where the reviewer bounces under a quarter of reports | capture and extraction, draft and submit, the filer's own submit gate, first-submission approval tile, support in the plan's own queue | usage and support: 12 drafted reports a month, i.e. $24 at full allowance (LC49), plan-queue support only | $2.00 per drafted report (LC49, LC48) | the 13th drafted report in a month, or a ticket the plan queue cannot close | low; the unit is the same meter, so a heavy account at the Good fence is an account EXP-2 and phase 3 will route to Better |
| Better | S-5, the 1,240 best-fit accounts with approval under 75% and 5 or more filers (N36) | everything Good adds: policy pre-check on the single top bounce cause (BL-05 slice, LC43), the reviewer's confidence flags (LEDGERLINE-S3), named review-path support | feature and support: SSO, custom policy rules, audit export, named support contact | $2.40 per drafted report, billed monthly in arrears, no minimum (N89) | an audit request for the export, an IT mandate for SSO, or custom policy rules | medium; the fence is features, not usage, so a mid-market account that never needs SSO parks happily at Better |
| Best | S-6, the 310 Enterprise accounts (N31), sales-led, outside EXP-1 | everything Better adds: retention of approval-gate records to 7 years (LC27), the DPA subprocessor addendum as a contract term accepted at D5, a named support rota | none above; this tier carries the capabilities | $2.40 per drafted report at EXP-2's terms, with an enterprise line quoted at roughly $2,400 MRR per account, an estimate on two prospects at the old seat price (N79); R4's DPA blocker closed at D5; the usage successor for N79 is out of scope until EXP-2 resolves | an enterprise IT requirement (SSO at scale), an audit scope Best was built for, or a deal desk quoting against a security review | none above; risk is the reverse, enterprise buyers reaching for Best's enterprise terms and paying Good's price for them; quote through the deal desk |

Prices step by $0.40, a 20% increase over the Good rung (about 17% off the Better rung), and the top rung is the revenue peak and margin guardrail in the same place: at $2.40 the margin is (2.40 minus 0.92) / 2.40 = about 62% (N89), above the 60% floor (N49); the lowest price clearing that floor is 0.92 / (1 minus 0.60) = $2.30 (LC51), which is why Good's $2.00 rung, at (2.00 minus 0.92) / 2.00 = 54% (LC51), cannot stand alone as the only price and can only stand as the bottom tier. The step is the survey's plateaus, not a round-number habit: $2.00 and $2.40 are the only two rungs within 5% of the revenue maximum, and the maximum is $2.40 (LC50); $2.80's plateau fails the margin guardrail at that cost.

### 3. Fences

| Fence | Type (feature / usage / seat / support) | Boundary | Why the customer accepts it | How it gets gamed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drafted reports included per month (12 at Good; unlimited at Better) | usage | good to better | The account already files about 7 reports a month at the median (N33), so the allowance covers a light account fully, and the account that crosses it is producing exactly the value the north star counts; the buyer predicts it from their own filing pattern | splitting one account into two so each stays under 12, a billing-system case the platform can see because accounts are the billing unit (N26); or gaming the meter with drafted reports that are later withdrawn, which M-003's share keeps visible |
| Support route (plan queue at Good; named review-path contact from Better up) | support | good to better | The S-5 buyer is the one whose reviewer bounces more than a quarter of reports (N35 against the segment rule); a named contact is what turns a bounce pattern into a fix, and Good users already get the plan's own queue | filing tickets through the free plan queue on questions that are really escalation requests, which ticket tagging on the add-on category (the N38 pattern) makes countable |
| SSO and custom policy rules | feature | better to best | Enterprise IT mandates identity (N31's sales-led accounts); a mid-market finance team has one approver and no identity vendor | an Enterprise account signing at Better and living without SSO until IT blocks it, which is a deal-desk problem, not a product fence, and the quote review should catch it |
| Audit export and 7-year approval-record retention | feature | better to best | The 11 legacy accounts using the audit export on the old plan prove the demand (the journey's migration finding), and the 7-year record rule (LC27) is the finance-records requirement Enterprise audits ask for, so the capability has to be purchasable somewhere | a Better account exporting monthly into its own store to simulate the record; a retention window the customer's own auditor can see, which the export's metadata carries |

No boundary carries more than two fence types: Good to Better is usage and support; Better to Best is features only (SSO and policy rules, and audit export and record retention, are the same feature class at two needs).

### 4. Tier-design checklist

- [x] Every tier names a segment and an upgrade trigger: Good, the 1,660 outside S-5 (LC3), at the 13th report; Better, S-5 (N36), at an audit or SSO need; Best, S-6 (N31), at an enterprise IT or audit scope
- [x] Good could be sold alone without embarrassment: it is the complete extraction-to-submission flow with the approval tile (LEDGERLINE-S2, S3, S5), priced where a light account files once a month for about $7.90 a month against a $149 plan (about $7.90 at the observed 47% drafted share, N90, N65), a product a satisfied small-team filer could stay on for years
- [x] Every must-be capability is in Good: the Kano survey surfaced no must-be class (its Open box), and the Kano drift warning lands on the policy line, so the sheet carries no fenced basic; the policy pre-check that the drift warning implies is the fence's first Better add, priced as a move the account makes when the pre-check becomes something it expects
- [x] No boundary uses more than two fence types: two at Good to Better (usage and support), one class at Better to Best (features)
- [x] Best contains at least one capability the demanding segment cannot do without: SSO, and the 7-year approval-record retention audit scope demands (LC27); both above the flat price's envelope
- [x] The price steps fit the Gabor-Granger plateaus, not a round-number habit: $2.00 and $2.40 are the plateau's two rungs (LC49), the step is $0.40, and the margin floor forces the top of the plateau, $2.40, as the price above the fence (LC51)
- [ ] The tier names describe the customer, not the price: not yet satisfied; names are held until the positioning refresh, because a good-better-best name read off the meter (Light, Team, Audit) describes the price and usage, which the checklist warns against; Maya Chen owns the naming pass with Tomas Lindqvist

**Decision rule, applied:** no customer loses a capability in use without a path to keep it: the 11 legacy accounts using the audit export (the journey's migration finding) get the audit capability, which the fence table places at Better, so the mapping rule is: audit export in use moves the account to Better at the successor usage price, held at their current spend for the remainder of the N93 term, so no account loses the export on move. The 7-year record retention Best carries is a scope the 11 accounts' exports did not demand, so it is not a loss; if an auditor asks for it mid-term, that is an exception, and exceptions go through Isabel Ferreira and into the decision log. Every price increase gets a stated notice period and a grandfather term: the 90-day notice and the 12-month hold above cover every cohort, and no account on the mapping list faces a mid-term price increase, so no account faces an increase without a notice period in the window.

## Reading the result

The fence table is non-empty at both boundaries, so the two lower rungs are two tiers, not one price with two faces: the $0.40 gap between $2.00 and $2.40 is the survey's plateau, which the framework's reading says is the ladder signalling price is not the lever inside the plateau, and the usage allowance plus the support route carry the boundary, exactly as the flat-plateau warning in the sheet's own example says to expect. Good passes the sold-alone test on arithmetic and on Kano: a 7-reports-a-month account pays about $7.90 at the observed 47% drafted share, well under the $149 plan it already budgets (N90, N65, N27), and the survey's whole acceptable range, $1.80 to $2.60, sits above the point Good prices (LC48), so the segment Good is for is not being baited. Best has SSO and the audit scope the Enterprise accounts asked for on their own evidence (N31, N79), so it is an anchor with named buyers rather than a decoy at the top of the menu. The real risk is the one the migration table names: the mapping run must happen against the real customer list before the successor price is announced, because the 73 accounts' real usage, not the S-1 median, decides who lands where, and the audit-export cohort needs its capability path written into the mapping before anyone reads a price list.
