## 2. Service level indicators

Phrase each SLI as good events divided by valid events, from the customer's side. "Valid" excludes maintenance windows and customer-caused delays. Four SLIs cover detection, estimation accuracy, notification latency, and the PSR-contact duty, the four failure points the DNO named as its highest regulatory exposure. Two to four SLIs is the range; four is the ceiling here because a fifth would dilute the owner's attention.

| SLI id | What is measured, in user terms | Good event | Valid event, with exclusions | Measured where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SLI-1 | Time between the earlier of first customer report or automated fault detection, and the moment Gridline's detection system confirms the event and opens an outage case | Good event: the outage case opens within the detection-window SLI threshold (see section 3) after the earlier timestamp. This measures detection speed, not restoration speed. | Valid event: every unplanned fault on an urban feeder that is confirmed as a genuine outage. Exclusions: planned work orders flagged in advance, customer-reported faults on private wires beyond the DNO's boundary, and events where the DNO's own SCADA/ADMS feed is down for scheduled maintenance (logged separately, not silently dropped). | Gridline Outage Event Ledger, field detection_confirmed_at, cross-referenced with dno_feed_timestamp and first_customer_report_timestamp |
| SLI-2 | Accuracy of the estimated restoration time Gridline publishes to affected customers, compared with the actual restoration timestamp | Good event: the published ETA is within the tolerance band of the actual restoration time (tolerance defined in section 3, target). A bad event is one where the customer waited longer than the ETA plus the tolerance, or the system was more than the tolerance band early. | Valid event: every confirmed unplanned fault on an urban feeder where an ETA was published to customers (i.e. every event that reached the customer-notification stage). Exclusions: events where the restoration was extended by an event outside the original scope (for example, a second fault discovered during the repair, logged as a new event), and events where the DNO's field crew flagged the ETA as provisional at the time of publication. | Gridline Outage Event Ledger, fields published_eta_timestamp and actual_restoration_timestamp |
| SLI-3 | Time from the moment Gridline's restoration event is confirmed to the moment the customer notification (SMS or email) is sent | Good event: the notification is sent within the notification-latency target (see section 3). This measures the trigger-to-send gap, not the restoration itself. | Valid event: every confirmed restoration event where a notification was due (the customer has a registered mobile or email and has not opted out). Exclusions: customer opt-outs, events where the customer's contact details are invalid in the DNO's CRM (logged and surfaced to the DNO's data-quality team rather than counted as a Gridline failure), and sends that fail due to a third-party SMS-gateway outage beyond Gridline's control (a distinct SLI if the DNO wants to track the gateway separately). | Gridline Notification Send Log, joined on the outage case ID in the Outage Event Ledger |
| SLI-4 | Whether the DNO's customer-care team successfully contacted the PSR customer within the contact-within window after an outage event affecting them | Good event: the PSR customer was contacted within the contact-within window (see section 3). A bad event is no contact recorded within the window, or a contact recorded after it. | Valid event: every PSR-registered customer whose address falls within the affected-customer boundary of a confirmed unplanned outage event. Exclusions: PSR customers with a valid "do not contact" preference recorded in the DNO's PSR register (the register is owned by the DNO, not Gridline; Gridline reads it, does not amend it), and cases where the DNO's phone system was down (logged separately). Gridline records the event trigger and the contact attempt; the contact outcome is a DNO-recorded field Gridline ingests. | Gridline Outage Event Ledger, joined with DNO PSR-contact outcome feed (the DNO owns the outcome field; Gridline records the trigger and timestamps the attempt) |

| SLI-2 | 90% of published ETAs land within the tolerance band of the actual restoration timestamp. ## 5. Error budget policy

The budget is the gap between the target and perfection over the window. For a 30-day rolling window at 30 days times 24 hours times 60 minutes equals 43,200 minutes, a 95% target on SLI-1 permits a budget of 2,160 failed-minutes-equivalents in the window, expressed as the number of events exceeding the 15-minute detection window: 5% of events. At the current small-sample rate of 13 of 14 events within window (1 of 14 exceeding), the observed excess-event count is 1, well inside the budget; but the budget is in event counts, not minutes, for SLI-1, because the SLI is event-based (an event either opened in time or did not), not minute-based.

For SLI-2, a 90% target on a 30-day rolling window with the sample rate of 11 of 12 within band (1 of 12 outside, about 8.3% outside, inside the 10% budget), the budget is the number of events outside the tolerance band permitted before the target is breached. For SLI-3, a 99% target over a 30-day window means the budget is 1% of due notifications sent outside the 5-minute window; on the current sample (14 of 14 within window, zero outside) the budget is unspent. For SLI-4, a 99% target means the budget is 1% of PSR-contact triggers logged outside the 1-hour window; the sample is too small to assess consumption.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Budget per window | SLI-1: 5% of events, i.e. up to 5% of confirmed urban-feeder events may exceed the 15-minute detection window before the target is breached. At 14 events (current baseline), that is 0.7 events permitted outside; since events are integers, effectively 1 event outside is within budget on a 14-event sample, but the budget scales with volume. SLI-2: 10% of published-ETA events may land outside the tolerance band. SLI-3: 1% of due notifications may be sent outside the 5-minute window. SLI-4: 1% of PSR-contact triggers may be logged outside the 1-hour window. |
| At 50% of budget consumed | Product manager and on-call engineering lead review the trend at the weekly ops standup. No action beyond monitoring. A single event exceeding the SLI-1 window on a low-volume month consumes the whole budget instantly (because 50% of 1 event permitted is 0.5 of an event, and one exceeded event equals 100% of the budget), so on low-volume months the 50% trigger is effectively the first event outside. This is a known property of low-count event-based SLIs; the review cadence below is set to catch it before it becomes a pattern. |
| At 100% of budget consumed (budget exhausted) | Feature freeze on the restoration pathway: no new routing, notification-template, or detection-threshold changes ship until the next SLO review. Reliability work is prioritized over feature work for the next sprint. The product manager raises a change request if a feature freeze would itself cause a regulatory-reporting gap (for example, if a planned notification-template update is needed to satisfy a new DNO reporting requirement), which is escalated to the DNO's regulatory reporting lead and the DNO's commercial contracts manager jointly. |
| Who decides exceptions | The DNO's regulatory reporting lead and the DNO's commercial contracts manager, jointly. Gridline's product manager (Ulla Karlsson) may recommend an exception but cannot grant one, because a budget breach on SLI-1 or SLI-4 has regulatory-filing implications (a filing that is later challenged may depend on whether Gridline's own targets were met or whether an exception was granted, and the DNO owns the filing). |
| Who has agreed to enforce this | Ulla Karlsson, product manager, 2026-09-11. Confirmation from the DNO's regulatory reporting lead and commercial contracts manager pending sign-off on this document's status change from "In review" to "Approved". |

Error budget is expressed in failed events for SLI-1, SLI-2, and SLI-4 (event-count SLIs), and in failed-notification counts for SLI-3. For SLI-3, at 99% availability over a 30-day calendar month, the budget is 1% of total due-notification sends. The exact number of sends per month is not yet known (the baseline sample does not separate notification volume from event volume); it will be stated at the first SLO review once a full month of production data is available. Do not quote a notification-count budget until that baseline exists.

## 6. Alert thresholds

Two alerts per SLO: a fast burn that pages, a slow burn that files a ticket. Every page links a runbook that exists in the DNO's and Gridline's operational documentation.

| Alert | SLO | Condition (burn rate or error rate) | Lookback window | Severity (page / ticket) | Routes to | Runbook |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SLI-1 fast burn | SLI-1 | One confirmed urban-feeder event opens an outage case beyond the 15-minute detection window, on a day where no major-event exclusion applies | Instant (event-level) | Page | Gridline on-call engineering lead; DNO outage desk | Gridline Runbook RB-01: detection-gap breach, steps to confirm whether the SCADA/ADMS feed was live at the earlier timestamp, and whether the event was genuinely unplanned (not a mis-classified planned work) |
| SLI-1 slow burn | SLI-1 | Cumulative event count exceeding the 15-minute window reaches 50% of the 5% budget in a rolling 30-day window | Rolling 30-day | Ticket | Gridline product manager (Ulla Karlsson); weekly ops standup agenda item | Gridline Runbook RB-02: detection-gap trend review, steps to pull event-level data from the Outage Event Ledger and compare against SCADA/ADMS feed logs |
| SLI-2 fast burn | SLI-2 | One published ETA lands outside the tolerance band on a confirmed event where the ETA was published as final (not provisional) | Instant (event-level) | Page | Gridline on-call engineering lead; DNO regulatory reporting lead (for awareness, not response) | Gridline Runbook RB-03: ETA-accuracy breach, steps to check whether the field crew's status updates were ingested correctly and whether a second fault was discovered without being re-logged as a new event |
| SLI-2 slow burn | SLI-2 | Cumulative published-ETA events outside the tolerance band reach 50% of the 10% budget in a rolling 30-day window | Rolling 30-day | Ticket | Gridline product manager (Ulla Karlsson); weekly ops standup | Gridline Runbook RB-04: ETA-accuracy trend review, steps to audit the ETA-calculation inputs against the field-crew data feed |
| SLI-3 fast burn | SLI-3 | One due notification is sent beyond the 5-minute window, or a notification is not sent at all when the SLI-3 conditions were met | Instant (event-level) | Page | Gridline on-call engineering lead; DNO customer-care team lead | Gridline Runbook RB-05: notification-latency breach, steps to confirm whether the event was confirmed in the ledger, whether the trigger fired, and whether the SMS/email gateway accepted the send |
| SLI-3 slow burn | SLI-3 | Cumulative notifications sent outside the 5-minute window reach 50% of the 1% budget in a rolling 30-day window | Rolling 30-day | Ticket | Gridline product manager (Ulla Karlsson); weekly ops standup | Gridline Runbook RB-06: notification-latency trend review, steps to audit trigger-to-send timing against gateway-provider delivery logs |
| SLI-4 fast burn | SLI-4 | One PSR-contact trigger is logged beyond the 1-hour window, or a PSR customer in the affected boundary has no contact attempt recorded within the window | Instant (event-level) | Page | Gridline on-call engineering lead; DNO regulatory reporting lead; DNO customer-care team lead (jointly: a PSR-contact gap is a licence-condition exposure, not just a support issue) | Gridline Runbook RB-07: PSR-contact trigger breach, steps to confirm the PSR register was read correctly, whether the contact attempt was logged, and whether the DNO's phone system was live at the time |
| SLI-4 slow burn | SLI-4 | Cumulative PSR-contact triggers logged outside the 1-hour window reach 50% of the 1% budget in a rolling 30-day window | Rolling 30-day | Ticket | Gridline product manager (Ulla Karlsson); DNO regulatory reporting lead (awareness, not action) | Gridline Runbook RB-08: PSR-contact trend review, steps to audit trigger records against the DNO's contact-outcome feed |

Runbooks RB-01 through RB-08 exist in the Gridline operational documentation repository as of this drafting. A runbook that does not exist is an alert that fires with no instruction; if any runbook in the table above has not been written by the first production traffic, the alert is disabled rather than left firing into a void.
