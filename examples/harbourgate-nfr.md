Kestrel's own committed SLA is 99.9% monthly availability (measured, N26); Marlowe's was 99.5% (measured, N27); Tidewater's 2021 contract carried no SLA clause at all (measured, N28). Quay sits in series with Kestrel (ADR-0003), so end-to-end availability is at most Kestrel's committed 99.9% times Quay's own: a 99.9% provider commitment leaves the payment-step target of 99.9% (N25) no error budget of its own, and an SLA commits Kestrel to service credits, not to availability. Whether N25 is meant to exclude Kestrel-caused minutes is not settled on the data sheet (open, owner Tomasz Wierzbicki, no date agreed). Until it is, that gap sits inside R4, accepted by name in D-022, rather than resolved by a new number, which is why R4 is carried here rather than left implicit. The recovery targets do not reconcile with the availability target either: a single post-sunset incident at the full 60-minute RTO (N29) alone uses more than a month's 99.9% budget, and the 30-minute drain RTO uses most of it; that tension is likewise not separately resolved and sits inside the same accepted risk.

## 3. Scale and capacity

| Dimension | Launch assumption | 12-month projection | Breaks at | Source of estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Payment requests per second | Average 6 req/s; peak minute 24 req/s, measured 2025-11-28 (measured, N24) | No demand projection is on the data sheet (open, owner Bea Lindqvist, no date agreed); the 50 req/s figure once shown in this column is Kestrel's per-account capacity limit, not a projection, and is moved to the note below | 80 req/s, where the payment table write path breaks (measured, load test 2026-06-05, N24), two months after this gate: see the revision log above | N24 as recorded (order data underlies the measured figures; the derivation is closed as of 2026-10-20 by N24a, see the note below); Kestrel MSA schedule 2; load test log |

The 6 req/s average and 24 req/s peak (N24) do not derive from a naive one-request-per-order model: N3's 6,400 orders/day is about 0.07 orders per second, and N4's 31,000-order peak day, spread evenly across 24 hours, is about 0.36 orders per second, far short of the measured 24 req/s peak taken alone. As of 2026-10-20, see the revision log above: N24a on the data sheet gives the requests-per-order multiplier by call type (authorise, status polls, retries, webhooks) that reconciles N24's average and peak against N3 and N4, with a stated peak-minute concentration factor; most of N24's measured volume is Quay's own clock-driven polling and reconciliation traffic, not order-driven calls. Kestrel's per-account client limit under MSA schedule 2, 50 req/s (also stated in N61 as the rate Quay enforces on itself, with 429 handling), sits 26 req/s above the highest measured peak and 30 req/s below where the write path breaks. That headroom is protected day to day, in aggregate, by Quay's own 50 req/s limit across every consumer and 429 handling, as api-contract section 5 implements it (N61). Per-consumer shares that sum to no more than 50 req/s are not sized (open, owner Bea Lindqvist). The peak change freeze (N64, 2026-11-13 to 2026-12-04) exists for a different reason, per D-022 and R10: to stop a self-inflicted outage from a Quay or flag change landing during peak trading while the legacy path sits under termination notice, not to protect request-rate headroom, since peak traffic arrives whether or not code changes.
