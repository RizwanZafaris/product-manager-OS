## 1. Product and data summary

- What the feature does and who uses it: Quay is the payment service Harbourgate's checkout calls to authorise, capture, void and refund card payments on the web, the app and the 61 in-store kiosks, replacing the nine-year-old checkout-pay layer for every customer who pays by card.
- Markets and jurisdictions in scope: the regimes cited in section 2, UK GDPR among them, are the ones this assessment answers against; sections below apply the same way across web, app and kiosk, since all three sit inside Harbourgate's own acquiring contracts with Kestrel, Marlowe and Tidewater as merchant of record. [OPEN: confirm whether Harbourgate trades in any jurisdiction beyond the one UK GDPR implies; not stated in the data sheet]
- Does the product contain an AI model making or influencing decisions about people? No AI or machine-learning model. Quay itself routes and reconciles payments and decides nothing about a customer. The fraud rules engine (I-9) that sits beside it consumes the decline event stream to score risk and flags a card-not-present order for step-up authentication above the £250 threshold or on a fraud-rule match (BR-005, N57, integrations I-9); it is a rule-and-threshold system, not a trained model, so the AI-and-financial-regulator overlay in `modules/regulated/` still did not activate (both halves of that gate are required: an AI or ML feature, and a financial regulator; only the second is true here). What the fraud engine's automated decision-making triggers instead is a UK GDPR DPIA question, answered in section 4, not the overlay. [skills/reg-gap-check/SKILL.md](../skills/reg-gap-check/SKILL.md) was still run on 2026-07-01, because Quay is a financial system even without a model. No separate output file exists in this example set, so its verdict is quoted here, not as a verbatim block but as the journey timeline's own words: reg-gap-check run, "module does not apply" (journey timeline, 2026-07-01), with Hamid Qureshi, Information Security Lead and PCI DSS owner, named regulatory owner for the obligations the overlay would otherwise have carried.

## 4. DPIA flag

- Systematic profiling or automated decisions with significant effects? Assessed against two systems, not one. Step-up authentication (BR-005) applies a fixed threshold Saoirse Whelan's team set and reviews, with a human-visible retry on a different method on failure (BR-001), not a silent decision; but under UK GDPR a rule-and-threshold system is not exempt from being "automated decision-making" just for lacking a scoring model, so the question has to be answered against the system that actually decides, not the one that merely triggers. That system is the fraud rules engine (I-9, DEP-6): it consumes the decline event stream, scores risk, and its flag is what forces step-up on an order that would otherwise clear (BR-005, N57); the data sheet describes this flagging and scoring, not a power to block or deny a payment outright without step-up, and no source cited in this document supports that stronger claim. [OPEN: Saoirse Whelan to confirm whether an engine flag can deny a payment outright, without step-up, which would change the ICO high-risk-list reasoning below]. On what is supported, the ICO's high-risk list covers data matching for fraud prevention, which describes this engine's scoring regardless of the open question above, since the engine matches decline-stream signals to a risk decision that determines whether a customer must clear an extra authentication step. **Yes**, on the fraud rules engine, on that basis; step-up itself (BR-005, AC-11) remains a fixed-threshold rule needing no separate DPIA, and this "yes" does not depend on the open question about outright denial.
- Large-scale processing of special category data? No. Section 3 lists no special category data; card and order data are ordinary personal data under UK GDPR.
- Systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area? No. The kiosks capture a payment attempt and its outcome, not customer behaviour or presence in the shop.
- New technology applied to personal data in a novel way? No. Hosted fields and a certified terminal SDK are established techniques from an accredited provider (DEP-1), not a novel use.
