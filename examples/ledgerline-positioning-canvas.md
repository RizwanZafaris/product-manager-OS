## What it is for

This is the working sheet behind the signed positioning document, [ledgerline-positioning.md](ledgerline-positioning.md). It positions the Ledgerline Expense Copilot for customer accounts already running Ledgerline Expenses.

Parts 1 to 4 were completed on 2026-10-29. Part 5 was deliberately held until 2026-10-30, creating one day between the customer and alternative analysis and the category decision. Sales first argued for the free bundle during that 2026-10-30 session. The delay broke the category anchor before the free-bundle frame could become the answer.

The commercial pass was opened by D1 on 2026-10-15: run a second pass for the add-on and reuse internal evidence only where a written reason says it transfers. The source trail is the [journey data sheet](ledgerline-journey.md) and its [coverage sheet](ledgerline-coverage-sheet.md).

## Run it when

- A product, feature or tier needs a positioning statement.
- Sales compares the add-on with the wrong alternative or with the right alternative on the wrong attribute.
- The team is choosing between an add-on frame, a broader category or a free-feature frame.
- Pricing and packaging need a category before the price is chosen.

This canvas was run before pricing and packaging. It does not reuse the positioning worksheet's stand-alone example.

**Arithmetic and decision rule:**

- S-5 is contained within S-1: 1,240 accounts out of 2,900 S-1 accounts.
- S-1 outside S-5: 2,900 minus 1,240 = 1,660 accounts, as recorded in LC3.
- S-5 treats both Part 3 values as urgent: 2 urgent values, both currently unproven.
- S-1 treats the in-platform value as urgent and the policy-line value as relevant but less urgent: 1 urgent value, 1 relevant value.
- S-6 has 0 evidenced urgent values in this pass.

S-5 ranks first because its approval threshold and filer threshold make the problem urgent, not because it is the largest segment. S-6 ranks last because the current evidence does not establish urgency for either value.

**Gate:** passed. S-5 is the single best-fit segment. S-6 is not combined with it as a second beachhead.

### Part 5: market category

Completed on 2026-10-30, one day after Parts 1 to 4. Sales first argued the free bundle in this session. D2 records the final decision not to bundle the copilot free into the Business plan.

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Category chosen | **Expense drafting add-on**, an add-on to the finance platform the S-5 account already runs. |
| What it makes them assume, before you say a word | It is a focused point tool that turns receipt information into a draft inside the existing Expenses workflow. They will expect a clear activation choice, a visible customer-policy check, a human review before submission and a separate commercial line rather than an unpriced platform feature. |
| Assumptions the category creates that we do not meet | The category can imply that every receipt is drafted correctly, that every language and receipt format is supported, and that no reviewer judgment is needed. We do not claim those things. The filer remains accountable, the reviewer remains part of the flow, and proof for the two positioning attributes was unproven on 2026-10-30. |
| Categories considered and rejected | **Expense management:** too broad. It makes the buyer expect the full system of record, workflow coverage and broad feature parity, while the distinctive value here is the draft inside the existing platform. **Receipt capture:** too narrow and too close to Cinderwick, which already owns that comparison in the sales conversation. **Free feature of the Business plan:** rejected under D2 because it hides the cost and signals that the value is zero. |

**D2 arithmetic recorded in the session:**

- Annual reports at the 60% drafted-share assumption: 2,900 accounts x 11 reports a month x 12 months = 382,800 reports.
- Drafted reports: 382,800 x 60% = 229,680 drafted reports.
- Receipts: 229,680 x 4.2 receipts per report = 964,656 receipts, about 965,000 receipts.
- Model cost: about 965,000 x $0.22 per receipt = about $212,000 a year.
- The estimated effect is about 1.9 points of Business-plan gross margin.
- Daniel Okafor's rule is that no feature costing more than one point of Business-plan gross margin ships without its own revenue line.
- Therefore, about 1.9 points is greater than one point, so the free bundle fails the stated rule and needs its own revenue line.

The free bundle also failed the category test: a free feature cannot credibly be positioned against Cinderwick's quoted $8 per seat per month offer while claiming that the same problem has commercial value.

**The swap test:** read the value row for each Part 3 attribute with the leading Part 1 alternative's name swapped in for ours.

1. Original: “The Ledgerline Expense Copilot shows the customer's matched policy line beside the suggested category before the filer submits.”
   - Swap with typing: “Typing into the Expenses form shows the customer's matched policy line beside the suggested category before the filer submits.” **False.**
   - Swap with spreadsheet: “A spreadsheet shows the customer's matched policy line beside the suggested category before the filer submits.” **False.**
   - Swap with Cinderwick: “Cinderwick shows the customer's matched policy line beside the suggested category before the filer submits.” **Not evidenced by the dated alternatives evidence, so the sentence cannot be treated as true.**
   - Result: the attribute remains in Part 2. Its customer value is still marked unproven.

2. Original: “The Ledgerline Expense Copilot is an add-on inside the Ledgerline Expenses workflow, using the account already active in that platform.”
   - Swap with typing: “Typing into the Expenses form is an add-on inside the Ledgerline Expenses workflow.” **False.** It is the existing workflow, not an add-on.
   - Swap with spreadsheet: “A spreadsheet is an add-on inside the Ledgerline Expenses workflow, using the account already active in that platform.” **False.**
   - Swap with Cinderwick: “Cinderwick is an add-on inside the Ledgerline Expenses workflow, using the account already active in that platform.” **False for the observed alternative.** N13 and N40 identify it as a separate quoted receipt-capture vendor that two design partners trialled and dropped.
   - Result: the attribute remains in Part 2. Its customer value is still marked unproven.

No Part 3 row reads true after the leading alternatives are swapped in. The swap test passes, with no row sent back to Part 2.

## Reading the result

All five parts pass their gates in order:

- Part 1 names three alternatives: typing into the Expenses form, a spreadsheet and Cinderwick.
- Part 2 identifies two attributes that the observed alternatives lack.
- Part 3 translates both attributes into customer value and keeps every proof row marked unproven at the date.
- Part 4 ranks S-5 first and S-6 last, with S-5 as the single beachhead.
- Part 5 chooses expense drafting add-on only after the one-day anchor break and rejects the broader and free-feature frames.

The sequence copies into [ledgerline-positioning.md](ledgerline-positioning.md). The category choice also records why D2 matters to positioning, not only to pricing: a free bundle would make the value claim inconsistent with the Cinderwick alternative.

## ILLUSTRATIVE example

This canvas is the Ledgerline example for the customer-facing Expense Copilot. It is not the stand-alone hypothetical from the blank worksheet.

The alternatives are typing into the Ledgerline Expenses form, a spreadsheet and Cinderwick. The unique attributes are the matched policy line shown before submission and the in-platform add-on flow. The best-fit segment is S-5, Business accounts with approval under 75% and 5 or more filers. S-6, Enterprise accounts, is last because the evidence does not establish the same urgency there.

Part 5 chooses expense drafting add-on, rejects expense management and receipt capture, and rejects the free-feature frame after the D2 arithmetic.

## The trap

The category was not chosen during the 2026-10-29 session. Parts 1 to 4 were completed first, then the sheet was closed for one day. On 2026-10-30, sales first argued for the free bundle. That argument was tested against the existing alternatives, the urgent segment and the margin rule rather than allowed to define the category.

The tell would have been a Part 1 containing only Cinderwick, or a Part 4 that selected the largest segment without a trigger. Instead, the sheet keeps typing, a spreadsheet and Cinderwick visible, ranks S-5 first and S-6 last, and records that both value claims remain unproven.
