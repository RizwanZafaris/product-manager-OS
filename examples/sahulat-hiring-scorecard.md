## 1. The seat

- **Team and product:** No product team exists yet. Sahulat Digital is a licensed electronic-money institution with about 140 staff and nine engineers reporting through Zainab Qureshi's line (N7, HR roster), and no product manager. This seat is the first one.
- **Why the seat exists:** No capacity plan exists to cite; the seat is the reason one will. Faisal Mirza has been making product calls himself alongside running the company, and the board's growth targets need someone who does discovery and definition as a full-time job rather than as a founder's Friday afternoon.
- **Stage-shift check:** per [the stage-shift card](../knowledge/roles/stage-shift.md), a 140-person company with zero product managers is still a search-stage company by product maturity, whatever its headcount says; Steve Blank's distinction is search versus execution, not staff count. The mismatch most senior hires die of here would be hiring someone who expects a scale-up's installed process, a research team, or a backlog someone else grooms. This rung, at this company, runs discovery, definition, support triage, and half of what a growth function would do, in the same week, exactly as the ladder's rung 2 entry names as the seed-stage variant, and exactly as Cagan's observation has it: the real product manager at a company this size is usually the founder, and the hire has to be someone the founder can actually hand that job to.
- **Owns on day 90:** one discovery pass run start to finish on the founder's own words for what is broken, ending at a Gate 1 recommendation, go or more discovery, not a shipped feature. · **Owns on day 365:** a product bet that has visibly changed the ledger, either through cash-in that stays as a balance or a transaction that replaces a cash-out, reported against a baseline set at hire.

## 3. Competencies and evidence

| Competency | Strong evidence sounds like | Weak evidence sounds like | Weight (high / medium) | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Runs discovery to a falsifiable conclusion, not a vibe | Names a specific method used to separate a real problem from a founder's preferred solution, the evidence that moved the conclusion, and a case where the evidence said something other than what was expected | "We talked to customers and validated the idea"; no method, no case where the answer came back no | high | screen, competency interview, work sample |
| Writes requirements at the weight the risk deserves, not the weight that feels thorough | Describes choosing a lighter document on purpose for a reversible call, and a heavier one for a one-way door, with the reasoning shown | Defaults to one document weight regardless of stakes, or cannot describe choosing a weight at all | high | work sample, work sample review |
| Kills their own bets | Names a bet they stopped, the evidence that moved them, and what stopping cost | "We pivoted", with no decision they made or evidence they found | high | screen, work sample review |
| Carries product risk alone in a small room, without a design or research team behind them | A concrete account of doing discovery, writing the spec, and reading the launch metric on one feature with no other product person involved, naming what they personally got wrong | Describes the same story with "we" throughout and no line the candidate alone owned | high | competency interviews, references |
| Works fintech-specific constraints into scope from day one, not after a compliance objection | Names a licensing, settlement, or fraud constraint from past work and how it shaped a design before anyone raised it as a blocker | Treats compliance and treasury as a review gate to get past rather than a constraint to design around | medium | competency interview with head of compliance |
| Reads a data pack for the real signal, not the headline number | Given a dataset, states what the pack does and does not support, and what a second cut would need to check before betting on the headline number | Repeats the headline figure as a conclusion with no check on what it excludes | medium | work sample, work sample review |

## 5. The work sample

- **Prompt:** critique a thin discovery document built from the same anonymised data pack every candidate receives (CN3), and recommend, with reasoning shown, whether the pack supports moving to a Gate 1 packet as written, needs one more evidence source before it can, or does not support the premise at all.
- **Time box and materials:** 2 to 3 hours, candidate's own time. Materials: the anonymised September 2025 work-sample data pack (CN3: 1,810,000 registered wallets; 398,000 30-day active wallets; 63 percent of cash-ins fully cashed out within 48 hours in Q2 2025, drawn from the 2025-08-31 ledger cut and anonymised for candidates), plus a one-page brief describing the founder's ask exactly as Faisal Mirza had been framing it internally.
- **Rubric:** one row per competency the sample tests, graded on the reasoning shown, not the memo's polish.
  - *Reads a data pack for the real signal:* strong answers note that a 63 percent pass-through rate proves a problem but not a solution, and ask what would separate "customers have no reason to keep a balance" from "customers have no product that gives them a reason"; weak answers treat the 63 percent as self-evidently pointing at any one feature.
  - *Requirements at the right weight:* strong answers propose a decision log entry or a one-pager for what is still reversible at this stage, not a full PRD; weak answers draft PRD-weight requirements against an unvalidated premise.
  - *Kills their own bets:* strong answers name at least one plausible feature the pack does not support and say so in writing, rather than defaulting to whichever idea the brief implied the founder wanted.

## 7. Decision

- **Decision:** Hire CAND-3, decided 2025-10-24, debrief 2025-10-22.
- **Evidence rows that decided it:** the discovery-to-falsifiable-conclusion row (a launch cancelled on customer evidence that contradicted the founder), the work-sample reasoning on the pack ("the pack tells you cash-in is not sticking, it does not tell you customers want to pay bills specifically"), and the carries-product-risk-alone account of a metric definition CAND-3 got wrong and then fixed.
- **Dissent:** head of compliance scored the fintech-constraints competency a 2, general fintech exposure without direct agent-network cash-in experience, and flagged it as the loop's one open risk rather than a blocker; the note carries forward into the new hire's first-90-days plan as a gap to close deliberately, not a surprise.
- **Calibration note:** scored against this rubric, not against CAND-2 or any earlier candidate. Next loop: split the fintech-constraints competency into two narrower rows (licensing and settlement mechanics versus agent-network operations specifically) so a strong general-fintech answer cannot mask a real gap in the second half, the way it nearly did here.
