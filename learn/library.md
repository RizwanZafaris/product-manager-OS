# The Library: what the cards compress

Knowledge cards are deliberately short; each one compresses a book or a body of practice into an essence, a skip-when, and a trap. This file points at the originals for when the compression is not enough. Every one-liner below is this repository's own words, never the author's; read the source for the author's.

Rules for this list:

- A book earns a row when a knowledge card or a path step draws on it, with two named exceptions in the last table. A library that catalogs everything teaches nothing, same rule as the [knowledge index](../knowledge/README.md).
- Read the card first, the book second. The card tells you the trap in one minute; the book earns its hours only after the card has made you curious or skeptical.
- Nothing here is reproduced. If you want the author's argument, buy the argument.

## Podcasts

Podcasts are commute learning, not evidence. When an episode changes your mind about something in your practice product, write the change into your `learn/products/<product>/PROGRESS.md` ledger; nothing from a podcast enters a real workspace without its own source.

| Show | Host | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Lenny's Podcast | Lenny Rachitsky | Named operators describing how they actually ran the methods the cards compress, failure modes included |
| Product Thinking | Melissa Perri | The organizational layer: product operating models, leadership, why good process dies in bad structure |
| Acquired | Ben Gilbert, David Rosenthal | Long-form company histories; the strategy entries (7 Powers especially) shown at work over decades |

## How to use this file from a path

A path step names its card; the card is mandatory, the book is not. Come here when a Done-when line keeps failing and the card's compression is the reason, or when the tutor's critique cites a card you found thin. Log what you read in your PROGRESS.md ledger with one line on what changed in your next attempt, because reading that changes nothing is decoration.
